A fixed pressure-coordinate LQGI direction for pressure-loss RTHS

The existing controller fails primarily at output regulation, not state-feedback stability. The defensible direction is one fixed integral observer-controller built on an experimentally validated pressure coordinate and certified in the complete deployed loop.

Develop the RTHS hypothesis, not a new LQG theory.

Integral-state LQG already exists in physical RTHS. Publication depends on validating the pressure coordinate and beating those baselines under identical broadband and complete-loop conditions.

How the literature handles stable but wrong tracking

Stability determines whether the response settles; the tracking mechanism determines where it settles. The distinction below separates exact-model tracking from robust rejection of unknown constant mismatch.

Where robustness enters the published RTHS controllers

The label “robust” covers different mechanisms. Only the LQG/LTR designs modify the ordinary LQG loop specifically to recover feedback margins.

PaperModification or additionHigh-level robustness conceptRiccati impactMain limitation
Zhou et al. · 2019 LQG/LTR feedback plus polynomial forward prediction. Recover the favorable full-state LQR loop shape; separately predict through actuator delay. Standard Riccati forms remain; LTR deliberately retunes the estimator weights. Improved margins do not create an internal model or guarantee zero offset under DC-gain error.
Fermandois · 2019 Proper inverse feedforward plus an LQG/LTR feedback regulator. Let the inverse track the reference while loop-shaped feedback rejects disturbances and sensor noise. Same LQG/LTR Riccati procedure; the inverse is an external two-degree-of-freedom block. Inverse accuracy limits tracking; nonminimum-phase behavior and delay restrict achievable bandwidth.
Phillips and Spencer · 2013 Model-inverse feedforward plus ordinary LQG feedback. Nominal model cancellation performs tracking; feedback corrects moderate model error and disturbances. No modified Riccati equation; conventional LQR and Kalman equations are used. Practical tolerance, not a formal uncertainty-set guarantee; persistent gain error can leave offset.
Zhang et al. · 2022 Fixed integral-state LQG plus online least-squares adaptive time-series prediction. The integrator corrects persistent error; the predictor relearns changing actuator delay. The control Riccati equation uses the integral-augmented plant; LQG gains remain fixed. Adaptation needs informative data and a suitable predictor; it is not certified for arbitrary plant change.
Condori Uribe et al. · 2023 Integral tracking-error state, augmented LQR, and a conventional Kalman estimator. Embed a constant-signal internal model so a stable equilibrium must have zero constant error. The control Riccati equation is enlarged by the integral state; the estimator Riccati equation is conventional. Rejects compatible constant mismatch, but does not guarantee stability for arbitrary dynamic uncertainty or delay.
Ou et al. · 2015 H∞ loop-shaping feedback, linear-quadratic estimation, and feedforward. Bound worst-case weighted disturbance amplification; use LQE for noise and feedforward for residual lag. The robust feedback is H∞ synthesis, not an LQR Riccati modification; LQE remains separate. Weight selection is demanding, the controller can be higher order, and the guarantee matches only modeled uncertainty channels.
Najafi and Spencer · 2019 Reference model, feedforward-feedback structure, and online adaptation law. Continuously change controller parameters so the uncertain plant follows chosen reference dynamics. No LQG Riccati modification; robustness comes from the adaptive law. Depends on adaptation assumptions, excitation, constraints, and the uncertainty structure.
Aguila et al. · 2024 Conditional adaptive time-series compensation added to baseline LQG. Adapt the feedforward prediction when delay behavior changes; retain LQG for feedback and estimation. No change to the LQG Riccati equations. Robustness is primarily to changing delay, not a general guarantee against stiffness-dependent DC-gain error.

Interpretation rule: additive-noise optimality, parametric robustness, delay adaptation, and robust zero-offset regulation are different claims. A controller may satisfy one without satisfying the others.

Fixed pressure-coordinate robust LQGI

The method uses one physical idea: pressure loss may drive the observed changes in dynamics, actuation, and measurement through one common coordinate. That hypothesis must first survive identification at three or more pressure conditions.

One candidate pressure coordinate

A₀ = (A₇ + A₅) / 2     A₁ = (A₇ − A₅) / 2
B₀ = (B₇ + B₅) / 2     B₁ = (B₇ − B₅) / 2
C₀ = (C₇ + C₅) / 2     C₁ = (C₇ − C₅) / 2

xₖ₊₁ = A₀xₖ + B₀uₖ + γξₖ(A₁xₖ + B₁uₖ) + wₖ
yₖ   = C₀xₖ + γξₖC₁xₖ + vₖ

One shared scalar ξ multiplies the complete secant. With only 5k and 7k, this is a model hypothesis—not evidence: any two points define a line. A common grey-box basis and a held-out pressure are required.

Why retain the secant diagnostic

The exact discrete secant is numerically rank six, while a margin-validated rank-three approximation reproduces the integral-loop stochastic radius to 6.5×10⁻⁷ relative error. This is a useful computation, not a novelty claim.

Classical LQG regulation

For one fixed specimen stiffness, the Riccati equation determines a feedback law that returns the system to its equilibrium while limiting control effort.

Consider the linear plant x˙=Ax+Bu. The state x represents departure from the desired equilibrium. An initial condition x(0)=x0 produces a transient response. The controller must suppress this response without demanding excessive actuator motion. This requirement is expressed by the infinite-horizon cost

J= ∫0∞ ( xTQx + uTRu ) dt

The matrix Q weights state error. The matrix R weights control effort. The optimization selects the complete input history u(t) that minimizes both penalties. Under the usual stabilizability and detectability conditions, the solution is the state-feedback law u=−Kx. It makes the closed-loop state approach zero. The cost accumulates the weighted error and effort throughout this return.

The gain follows from the continuous-time algebraic Riccati equation

ATP +PA − PB R−1 BTP +Q=0 K= R−1 BTP

The plant matrices A and B are specified. The weights Q and R are also specified. The symmetric matrix P remains unknown. For n states, it contains n(n+1)/2 independent unknowns. The Riccati equation solves for them. The feedback gain K is then obtained directly. The minimum cost for a given initial condition is J*=x0TPx0.

This regulation result supplies the control part of LQG. Full-state feedback requires every component of x. In practice, the available signal is often the measured output. The stochastic model introduces process noise w and measurement noise v:

x˙=Ax+Bu+Gw y=Cx+v

A Kalman filter uses y to construct the state estimate x^. The implemented controller becomes

x^˙ =Ax^+Bu +L(y−Cx^) u=−Kx^

The steady-state estimation covariance Σ satisfies a second Riccati equation:

AΣ+ΣAT +GWGT −ΣCTV−1CΣ =0 L=ΣCTV−1

The two matrix unknowns are therefore the control solution P and the estimator covariance Σ. The feedback gain K follows from the first solution. The estimator gain L follows from the second.

Add specimen stiffness to the cost

Write α = ks/ks0, where ks is the current specimen stiffness, ks0 is its initial stiffness, α = 1 is the undamaged condition, and αmin is the lowest stiffness retained in the model.

J(K)= ∫ αmin 1 p(α) ∫0∞ ( zαT Q zα + uαT R uα ) dtdα

The inner integral is the familiar LQGI cost at one fixed residual stiffness α. The new outer integral repeats that evaluation over the stiffness interval and combines the results using p(α).

What the added integral means

The mathematical change is small but precise: K is no longer selected from only one plant. The same K must act on every stiffness-dependent augmented model Aa(α), Ba(α).

The role of p(α)

p(α) is the stiffness weighting. If it is uniform, every residual stiffness is valued equally. If it is concentrated near small α, the design gives more importance to damaged conditions. It is normally normalized so that its integral from αmin to 1 equals one.

The role of one common K

For each α, zα and uα follow a different plant response, but the feedback law remains uα = −Kzα. The optimization changes K until the weighted sum of all these time-domain costs is minimized.

In practice, approximate the outer integral using stiffness samples αi and quadrature weights wi:

J(K) ≈ ∑i=1N wi Jαi (K)

This is generally not solved by inserting the outer integral into one ordinary CARE, because Aa(α) and Ba(α) vary with stiffness. Instead, one searches for a common K while evaluating the cost of that same gain at all selected stiffness values.
