The numerical substructure echoes dm straight back into dd — and the OSI hears itself

Closing the numerical loop does not change the PID + Hdc,dm loop — the intuition is right. What it changes is the command signal the OSI listens to: the 3-DOF FEM has a direct feedthrough term dd = C·x + interface_from_force·fm, so every sample of the measured force — noise included — reappears in dd within the same tick, amplified ×8 by the fem3_ncf3 phase lead. Above the FEM's top mode (18.5 Hz) that echo is almost all of dd. The estimator then measures the inverse of the outer feedback path instead of the inner-loop FRF, the chordal margin at those bins reads 0.1–0.3, and the authority law dutifully sheds to the floor. One subtraction at the OSI's command tap — estimator untouched — restores the margin to where the open-loop tests always had it.

Where the echo enters the loop

The RTHS signal path, with the part nobody drew on the whiteboard in red. The FEM's output equation has a D-term: interface_from_force = −5.22·10⁻⁸ m/N. Multiply by the interface stiffness (≈6·10⁶ N/m) and the phase lead's high-frequency gain (×8) and the loop hands the OSI a zero-lag copy of roughly −0.23·dm at every frequency, buried inside dd:

The proof is one filter and one regression away. Take the >25 Hz part of dd during the earthquake and overlay the reconstructed echo G_lead(iff·fm): they are the same waveform (r = 0.94), and the cross-correlation peaks at 0.0 ms — the FEM's algebraic D-term, not the actuator's forward path, which would show up 15–25 ms later:

Why the estimator cannot defend itself

The OSI's ST branch estimates the inner-loop FRF as M = S_uy/S_uu from (dd, dm) and takes the worst chordal margin over bins that pass three gates — coherence, input power, sidelobe. This is textbook-correct when the command is exogenous. The echo breaks the assumption, and it breaks it in a way every gate approves of:

  • Coherence gate: the echo is a filtered copy of dm — γ² sits at 0.8–1.0 across 20–100 Hz. Passes.
  • Input-power gate (−35 dB): el-centro through the FEM concentrates below ~20 Hz; the genuine 3–10 Hz bins sit at −26…−31 dB below the dd peak while the echo floor sits at −20…−35 dB. The echo bins are often stronger than the genuine ones — no threshold separates them. Passes.
  • What M becomes: at echo-dominated bins the cross-spectrum measures the inverse of the outer feedback path, not the plant. M gets dragged to ≈ −0.5…−2 on the negative-real axis; the chordal margin of that fiction is 0.1–0.3. The min-scan then reports it as "the loop" — at 46 Hz, b reads 0.20 where the plant model says 0.52.

This also explains why the BLWN / band-noise tests were always fine. An injected broadband command puts real exogenous power in every bin; the echo (a fraction of dm's noise floor) is negligible next to it, the same estimator reads 0.6–0.7, and everything looks healthy. The echo only owns the band when the exogenous content is narrowband and low — exactly an earthquake at intensity 0.06. And it explains the characteristic time signature you saw: at EQ onset the estimator's first windows are nearly pure echo (fast drop to ≈0.1), then the strong motion adds genuine low-frequency bins that pull the min-scan up to the least-bad echo bin (recovery to only ≈0.3).

No parameter can fix this. We checked every gate knob: the echo is coherent (γ²→1), often stronger than the genuine excitation (power gate inverted), broadband (frequency-median can't vote it out), and stationary (regime detector never fires). The information the estimator needs — "which part of dd did the plant not cause?" — simply is not in (dd, dm). It has to come from the wiring.
FIX

Subtract the echo at the OSI tap — one gain, one sum

The FEM computes dd = C·x + iff·fm. Both iff (a FEM constant) and fm (the measured force the FEM already consumes) are sitting in the model, so the echo term is known exactly, sample by sample. Give the OSI's command input the strictly-proper part only; the actuator keeps receiving the full dd — RTHS physics is untouched, and the estimator, runtime, and authority law stay byte-for-byte as they are.

Simulink wiring (exact)
u_osi = G_T( dd_fem − iff·fm ) = G_T(C·x)

Tap the FEM output before the phase lead, subtract iff·fm (one Gain + one Sum), pass that through a copy of the phase-lead block, feed the OSI's dd port. Zero approximation: it reconstructs the FEM's strictly-proper output.

Equivalent post-lead form: u_osi = dd_lead − G_T(iff·fm).

Offline replay (this study)
u_osi = dd − s·G_T(iff·fm), s ≈ 0.92 (fitted)

Records only store the post-lead dd, so the echo is reconstructed from fm and the saved fem3_ncf3 lead, with one scalar s fitted on the >25 Hz band (the record does not resolve an extra rig-side scaling on fm; on-target the structural wiring needs no fit). 10.8 dB echo suppression — enough to push every echo bin under the estimator's own gates.

Three cross-checks worth calling out:

  • The classifier agrees with reality. Every record named RTHS with the feedthrough wiring shows the 0.9+ zero-lag signature; every injected-signal test shows either no correlation or the −52-sample forward-path lag. Nothing was hand-labeled.
  • The fix does not mask real danger. In RTHS_instability_during_eq the cleaned margin still dives below 0.1 exactly at the divergence — the y-signal (dm) is untouched, so the residual and burst branches see the same growth they always did, and genuinely excited bins still carry the ST evidence.
  • Open-loop behavior is bit-identical. When no echo is detected nothing is subtracted; in the Simulink wiring the correction term is literally zero when the numerical loop is bypassed.
ARM

The start-of-test drop: hold when there is no evidence

Even with the cleaned tap, the first seconds after the numerical loop engages read low (b ≈ 0.2 for ~5 s on RTHS_02): the 5 s analysis window still straddles the switch-on, and during the EQ ramp-in the FEM's modal response to force noise outweighs the tiny exogenous content. That reading is not evidence — and the authority law already has the right reflex for "no evidence": when the margin is NaN, it holds (fable_auth.m, "no evidence: hold"). So the remaining piece is a small evidence-arming gate (fable_evidence_gate.m) that decides, from the OSI's own input signal, when the slow margin deserves to be believed:

  • present — trailing 2 s RMS of the (cleaned) command tap above theta = 0.05 mm (ramp-in/idle sit at 0.002–0.04 mm, strong motion at 0.26–0.79 mm);
  • armed — present continuously for arm_s = 6 s, one full analysis window plus the margin median span, so everything the estimator remembers was produced under real excitation;
  • fade blank — if the 1 s RMS falls below half the 6 s RMS (an excitation phase ending abruptly), the margin is blanked for 4 s without disarming: the window is full of stale strong segments and dips spuriously right there. Only falling excitation blanks — a growing divergence can never blank itself;
  • while not armed the law receives b = NaN → authority holds. Fast trips are deliberately not gated: fable_auth processes them before the NaN hold, so the burst/FRF guards keep watching — including a divergence that starts from an idle loop, which raises the excitation measure and arms the gate by itself.

The price, measured. Genuine events during armed operation shed on time (instability_at_the_end, event at 87 s: −0.5 s vs ungated). A genuine event inside the very first arming window (instability_during_eq: event 3 s after engagement) sheds ~11 s later — during the first seconds of excitation, "healthy loop under new input" and "diverging loop" are statistically the same from (u, y), so this delay is an information limit, not a tuning miss; the rig's displacement/force interlocks remain the backstop for that window. The theta/arm sweep (0.03–0.08 mm × 5–8 s) confirms the onset protection is robust: every combination except the loosest (0.03 mm / 5 s) keeps start-up authority at 1.00.

Simulink realization: RMS block (2 s) → compare (0.05 mm) → 6 s on-delay with the 1 s/6 s fade comparator → a switch that publishes NaN in place of the slow margin while unarmed (or gates the authority-update enable). Plain blocks, no algorithm change; validated in run_evidence_gate_validation.m.
LIM

Limits, and what to do at the rig

What the fix does not cover

  • Engagement transient (~5–7 s). When the numerical loop switches on, the 5 s analysis window briefly mixes silence with earthquake, and during the EQ ramp-in the FEM's dynamic (modal) response to force noise — which no D-term subtraction can remove — still outweighs the tiny exogenous content. Handled by the evidence gate (section ARM): the margin is blanked and authority holds until the excitation has been present for a full window.
  • Closed loop idling without excitation. If the numerical loop runs while the earthquake is essentially zero (record: RHTS_no_inst_butindicators_jump), the only signal circulating is noise — there is no exogenous information at any tap, and no (dd, dm) estimator can measure a margin. Also handled by the gate: it simply never arms, and authority never moves.
  • Cold-start events. A genuine divergence within the first ~6 s of excitation sheds late (~11 s on instability_during_eq) — an information limit of any spectral estimator; the rig's amplitude interlocks are the backstop for that window.
  • Records already ruined by the live collapse. Two of the saved runs executed with authority at 0.08 — the actuator wasn't tracking, so the "plant" in the data genuinely isn't the healthy loop. Offline replay can't (and shouldn't) make those look healthy. They need to be re-run after the wiring fix.

Recommended order of work

  • Add the tap correction in model_ColumnSetup_00: u_osi = G_T(dd_fem − iff·fm) (Gain + Sum + one copy of the phase-lead block). The OSI/authority runtime is untouched.
  • Add the evidence-arming gate (section ARM) on the same tap: RMS → threshold → on-delay → NaN switch; defaults theta 0.05 mm, arm 6 s, fade ½ × (1 s / 6 s), blank 4 s.
  • Shadow-run one RTHS (authority logged, not applied) to confirm the live margin sits at ≈0.6–0.7 like the replay, then re-enable active authority.
  • Separate topic, worth deciding deliberately: on the 5k specimen the healthy margin (≈0.35 by the plant model) is below the current 0.55 target, so the law will detune that configuration by design. Either accept that or run the 5k tests with a lower target.
