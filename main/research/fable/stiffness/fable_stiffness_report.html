RTHS specimen identification · research/fable/stiffness

A smoother, more reliable way to estimate specimen stiffness — and finally its mass and damping

The current online estimator fits F = m·a + c·v + k·x by total least squares on time-domain signals. It tracks stiffness passably but jitters, and in the field it does not really recover mass or damping at all. This note proposes fable_kmc, a causal frequency-domain errors-in-variables tracker, and measures both against nonparametric references on four recorded runs.

1 · How stiffness is estimated today

The specimen is modelled as a single-degree-of-freedom oscillator

F(t) = m·a(t) + c·v(t) + k·x(t)

where x is the measured displacement dm (in metres), F is the measured force fm, and v, a are its first and second time derivatives. The current online estimator (estimate_tse.m) stacks [a v x 1 F] and takes the smallest right singular vector — a total-least-squares (TLS) fit that treats every column as noisy. The pending Simulink replacement (windowed_noise_whitened_tls.m) is the same fit on short causal windows, noise-whitened and smoothed by a small Kalman filter. Both share one structural choice: they work in the time domain, and they build v and a by differentiating the displacement.

2 · Where the current method breaks

Three problems show up directly in the recorded data.

2.1 · Mass and damping are not actually recovered

Differentiating a 30 Hz-filtered displacement twice to get acceleration destroys most of the high-frequency content that carries the mass term m·a. The offline TLS/LSE batch fits land near 26–28 kg against a specimen mass of 44.1 kg — roughly 36% low. Worse, the estimates logged by the live rig (m_hat, c_hat) sit pinned at their floor — about 0.45 kg and 0.24 N·s/m — meaning the field system is really only tracking k and reporting placeholder mass and damping.

2.2 · A pure viscous model forces damping negative

This rig has appreciable hysteretic (structural) damping — a quadrature term that is roughly flat in frequency rather than proportional to it. Forcing the two-parameter viscous form c·v to absorb it drives c negative (fable with the h₀ term disabled returns c ≈ −1.9×10³ N·s/m). A negative damping estimate is not just cosmetic; it is unusable for any downstream stability or energy argument.

2.3 · The track jitters

Because each short window is fit independently and then lightly smoothed, the stiffness track wanders. Measured as the 95th-percentile step-to-step change (per 0.25 s), the current and pending methods move 6–45% of the stiffness level; the windowed LSE reaches 74% on the stress record. That jitter is the reason a Kalman filter was bolted on — it treats a smoothing problem as a filtering problem, after the fact.

3 · The proposed method (fable_kmc)

fable_kmc keeps the same physical model but estimates it where differentiation is exact and noise is separable: the frequency domain. The frequency-response function from displacement to force, H(ω) = F(ω)/X(ω), of an SDOF specimen is

H(ω) = k − m·ω² + j·( h₀ + c·ω )

Every term is linear in the parameters, and the derivative operators became exact multiplications (v→jωX, a→−ω²X) — no finite differencing, no differentiation noise. The pipeline, run causally every hop_s seconds:

  • Rolling cross-spectra. One Hann-windowed FFT of the newest seg_s segment updates exponentially-forgotten averages of Sxx, Sff, Sxf with memory mem_s. Smoothing happens here, on the sufficient statistics — the right place for it — not on the parameters after the fact.
  • Noise-debiased FRF. H = Sxf / (Sxx − Nxx), where Nxx is the per-bin displacement noise floor measured from the quiet start. Subtracting it removes the errors-in-variables bias that otherwise pulls any regression of F on x toward zero stiffness.
  • Informative-bin selection. Bins are kept only inside the fit band and only where coherence, displacement SNR, and a resonance guard all pass. Band-limited excitation therefore shrinks the working band automatically instead of poisoning the fit.
  • Robust, information-weighted fit. The four parameters [m, c, k, h₀] are fit by weighted least squares, each bin weighted by the inverse variance of its own FRF estimate (a function of coherence), then re-weighted by a Huber pass so local resonances or harmonic distortion cannot dominate. Relative ridge priors gently hold the weakly-observable parameters toward their previous value when the excitation stops informing them — without damping the data-driven k.
  • Fast adaptation on genuine change. A short-memory spectra set watches the FRF shape. When the newest data disagrees with the slow average beyond its own noise (a real plant change, not merely a change in drive level), the slow statistics are discounted once so the tracker re-converges in a fraction of mem_s.

Each estimate carries a 1-σ covariance from the fit and a status flag (acquiring / tracking / hold), plus a model-free low-band diagnostic k_direct = median Re H for a sanity cross-check.

4 · Why the frequency domain — and why h₀

The chart below is the measured FRF of record 1708 over its trim window, with fable's fitted model on top. The real part carries k (its low-frequency value) and m (its downward curvature). The imaginary part is the tell for the damping model: the data sits near a flat ≈4×10⁵ N/m, the signature of hysteretic damping. A purely viscous term c·ω with the small c the specimen actually has would be nearly zero here (lower line) — which is exactly why forcing the viscous-only model makes the fit throw c negative to compensate.

8 · Stress test: changing excitation

The play record splices one physical run into five 32-second blocks and scales the force channel by [1, 0.5, 1, 0.25, noise] with a drifting offset — a deliberately corrupted signal. The specimen never changes, so the "right" answer is a constant k; but with the force channel scaled, every method reads the apparent secant stiffness of the block. The point is not accuracy here — it is how gracefully each method moves and recovers. Fable's fast-adaptation marks (dashed red on the track) fire at the two largest transitions.

9 · Honest limits & the k-ambiguity

  • "True" stiffness is band- and model-dependent at the ~7% level. On record 1708 the low-band FRF reads 6.71 MN/m, fable reads 7.09 MN/m, and the official specimen-ID value is 7.20 MN/m. These disagree because a real column is not a perfect SDOF: the measured Re H is not flat at low frequency, so the intercept you call "k" depends on the band and the curvature model. Debiasing the noise moves the low-band anchor only 0.7% (the low-band SNR is high), so that is not the explanation — the spread is physical. Fable agrees with the whitened-TLS specimen value to 1.5% and recovers mass to under 1%, which is the strongest available cross-check; but no method here is "5% wrong" in an absolute sense, and the report scores accuracy against the low-band anchor only because it is model-free.
  • Observability is honest, not free. Mass needs high-frequency excitation; damping needs the mid-band. When the command stops driving part of the band, those parameters are held by their priors rather than invented. Under genuinely band-limited or corrupted excitation (the low-force play blocks) the stiffness follows the secant the data actually supports — as it must.
  • Not yet code-generation-ready. The pending Simulink path targets the whitened-TLS estimator, which is already fixed-size and codegen-safe. fable_kmc uses per-hop FFTs and an eigen/solve step; porting it to the Speedgoat would need fixed buffers and a fixed bin count, following the same staged verification the OSI work used. This note establishes the offline case first.

10 · Files & how to run

  • fable_kmc.m — the estimator. out = fable_kmc(x, F, fs, cfg), x in metres, F in newtons.
  • fable_kmc_config.m — every tunable parameter, documented inline.
  • run_fable_kmc.m — interactive tuner: load a record, edit any knob, Calculate, watch the k/m/c tracks and confidence bands update.
  • compare_stiffness_methods.m — the harness behind this page; writes results/compare_results.mat and the .js this report reads.
