Recommended research hypothesis

One extra output: make the response insensitive to the specimen

The proposal keeps a fixed H∞ controller and adds one mathematically explicit performance output. It does not require an online estimator, switching law, neural network, predictor, or bundle of robust-control techniques.

Why this idea

The available model already tells us how the plant moves from 7k to 5k. Penalizing the derivative of the closed-loop response uses that information at the exact point where the current designs struggle.

Possible contribution statement

A specimen-sensitivity performance output for fixed H∞ control of stiff RTHS actuator–structure interaction, validated across identified endpoints and a blind intermediate condition.

The existing parametric model gives the derivative

No learned surrogate is necessary. Differentiate the identified state-space transfer with respect to its dimensionless specimen coordinate.

G(s,θ)=C(θ)[sI−A(θ)]−1B(θ)+D(θ) Rs=[sI−A(θ)]−1

For the current affine diagnostic family, Aθ and Cθ are simply the endpoint secants, while Bθ=0. The same derivation works after a better physical family is identified.

Honest limitation

Fixed-order H∞ tuning with a repeated controller and a continuous parameter is generally nonsmooth and nonconvex. The equation is sound, but a numerical solution is not a proof of global optimality. Use multiple starts and independent dense-parameter verification.

Keep the actuator limit separate and visible

A standard H∞ norm cannot promise the maximum of a step trajectory. The optimization must check the family’s raw command directly.

maxθ∈[0,1],t∈[0,T]|ustep(t;θ,K)|−2.5≤10−2

The specification is still 2.5; 10−2 is the accepted feasibility tolerance. Report the worst (θ,t), raw peak, saturated peak, and whether the solution is tolerance-active. This separates a real physical requirement from a soft command weight.

“Better than PID” needs a frozen comparison

The PID is not inserted into the proposed equation. It is the success threshold, evaluated through exactly the same deployed architecture.

Predeclare the score: lower worst-condition tracking error and response spread than PID, with no worse command peak, saturation exposure, or stability failure. Show each condition separately and the worst case; a mean can hide one bad endpoint.

The decisive experiment is small

Only one switch—λ—is needed to establish whether the proposed term adds value.

  1. Identify an intermediate condition and fit the specimen coordinate; reserve a different intermediate condition as a blind holdout.
  2. Freeze controller order, ordinary weights, solver settings, random starts, and the 2.5 command constraint.
  3. Tune the baseline with λ=0.
  4. Tune a short, declared sweep of λ>0; select before opening the blind holdout.
  5. Compare PID, ordinary H∞, and the one-term design at 7k, 5k, the holdout, and the complete nonlinear RTHS loop.
  6. Reject the novelty hypothesis if the added term does not improve worst-condition tracking/consistency under the same command budget.

Why this is publishable if it works

The ablation isolates a physically motivated mathematical change; the holdout tests whether the model generalizes; and the command constraint prevents a false performance win. That evidence is more defensible than a large optimizer with many untraceable ingredients.

Two simple fallback ideas—test separately

If the parameter-derivative term fails, do not immediately combine more methods. Change one assumption at a time.

A

PID-envelope tracking weight

Fit the ordinary H∞ error weight to the worst PID error envelope, with a finite floor near PID zeros. This directly asks the design to beat the benchmark, but uses less specimen physics.

B

Physics-scaled break frequency

Scale the tracking-weight bandwidth with ωn=keff/meff so the desired response is constant relative to the moving structural mode.

Neither fallback should be stacked with the primary term until its independent contribution is measured. Simplicity is part of the research design, not merely an implementation preference.
