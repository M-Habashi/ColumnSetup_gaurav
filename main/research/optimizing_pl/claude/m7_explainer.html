The premise

Why the output knows what the input can't

Near the stability edge the loop grows a tall sensitivity peak at about 37 Hz. The closed loop amplifies its own internal noise — servo hum, friction, quantization — right there, so the resonance shows up in the measured displacement dm even when the test input never visits 37 Hz.

That growth is the incipient instability. So the output carries a warning the input physically cannot deliver — which is exactly why M7 can stay blind to the command and still see trouble coming.

The measurement

One number from the spectrum

Take a short window of dm. Compare the energy in the resonance band to a quiet reference band off to the side:

Spk = sqrt( peak PSD over 25–42 Hz / median PSD over 55–75 Hz )

A flat spectrum gives Spk ≈ 1. A sharpening 37 Hz peak drives it up. The floor band sits above the 60 Hz line so the ratio tracks the resonance, not the overall noise level.

The conversion

From a peak to a margin, in three steps

01

The peak is the sensitivity

The output PSD near resonance is |S(jω)|² times the internal noise. So the peak/floor ratio is the height of the sensitivity peak itself.

Spk ≈ ‖S‖∞  (peak sensitivity)
02

Peak sensitivity is closeness to −1

‖S‖∞ is the inverse of the closest the loop comes to the −1 instability point. The NCF margin is that distance, normalized — so the margin is inversely proportional to the peak.

‖S‖∞ = 1 / min|1+L|  ⟹  b ∝ 1 / Spk
03

Pin the constant on a healthy start

Assume the model is right at the beginning. Take the median Spk over the first 7.5 s as the reference and the model's binding-band margin bnom = 0.588. Healthy reads bnom; as the peak grows, b falls.

b(t) = bnom · Spk,ref / Spk(t)

Because the constant is calibrated, M7 is a relative reading — it needs the healthy reference. That is the price of being output-only. A companion method that estimates b straight from the closed-loop S and T reads it absolutely, but needs the band excited.

Why output-only

Where the FRF methods get fooled

FRF methods

Locked to a false alarm

Under a low-frequency input, the open-loop FRF dm/dc collapses to the tautology −1/C at 37 Hz — it reports a near-zero margin even when the loop is perfectly healthy.

Method 7

Reads the resonance directly

M7 ignores the input, so the lock can't fool it. It reads the 37 Hz resonance in dm — and is the only indicator that still detects when the input has no energy up there.

Speed

Why it lags — and how to make it fast

M7's resolution comes from a multi-second PSD buffer, so the reading lags by roughly half the buffer, and the resonance itself takes a few cycles to build. That is a configuration choice, not a law.

Swap the block PSD for a recursive narrowband tracker — a bandpass at 37 Hz plus an exponential power estimate — and Spk updates every sample with ~0.2–0.5 s of memory instead of seconds. The real floor is physics: telling a 37 Hz tone from noise needs only a few of its cycles, about 0.1 s.
