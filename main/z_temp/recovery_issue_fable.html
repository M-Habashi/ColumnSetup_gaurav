Why authority never came back — and is Codex's fix the right one?

Codex's report (z_temp/GDBqFpKb_recovery_debug.html) diagnoses why the authority factor a stayed at 0.2 after the diagnostic gain returned to nominal, and proposes a "diagnostic gain edge" restore branch inside the authority law. This page re-derives its diagnosis from the raw record, replays the record through the current runtime, re-runs the 10-case suite fresh, and gives a verdict.

TLDR. Codex's diagnosis is correct — I reproduced it bit-for-bit. Two of its three fix layers (feasible-target clamp + blind probe-and-verify) are already committed at HEAD and do resolve the deadlock (recovery in ~21 s). Its headline proposal — wiring the test bench's gain signal into the production authority law — I would not adopt: same ~1 s restoration is available from the test harness side with zero production risk. And the fresh suite run exposes what its "no-regression" replay missed: the knob that was turned to make recovery fast (latch_decay_tau_s 200→1 on 07-03) broke the quiet-band defense (case 4, 51% violation). Restoring it to 120 s fixes case 4 while the blind probe still recovers this record.

1 · What actually happened in the record

The test ran with target_outer_ncf = 0.8 and up_deadband = 0.10. When the gain hit 2×, the margin truly collapsed (0.27–0.40) — a burst trip at 64.402 s plus severe sheds took a to the 0.2 floor by 65.277 s. Correct behavior so far. The failure is what happens after the gain returns to 1 at 75.469 s:

Lock 1 — impossible arithmetic

Recovery requires the filtered margin ≥ target + up_deadband = 0.90. But the indicator is capped at 0.7071 by construction. No measurement could ever unlock recovery — the config asked for a margin that cannot exist.

Lock 2 — the evidence disappears

The margin does read healthy (at cap) from 77.777 s — 2.3 s after the gain return, the analysis-window flush; that delay is physics, not a bug. But the earthquake record ends right after: from 78.402 s the evidence gate blanks b_osi to NaN, and the law as of 07-06 had only one behavior for NaN: hold forever.

Either lock alone is fatal; this record had both. With a feasible threshold the healthy window was still only ~0.6–2.3 s long (and, in the production replay below, additionally blocked by the post-shed cooldown until 0.25 s after the gate blanked). So any real fix must handle Lock 2, not just Lock 1.

2 · Codex's solution, explained plainly

The report is dense, but its proposal is three separable layers:

A — decide against a feasible reference

Keep the requested target for reporting, but make authority decisions against b_ref = min(target, cap − up_deadband − 0.02), so the recovery threshold is always below the cap. Status: already committed — prepare_fable_runtime now clamps (with a warning), and the production config moved to target 0.55 / up_deadband 0.04 (threshold 0.59).

B — never fake the measurement

Leave b_st, b_res, b_fast, b_osi untouched: when evidence is gone, show NaN / WARMING, never synthesize a healthy 0.7071. Status: uncontroversial, matches the current design.

C — the actual proposal: a diagnostic-restore branch in the law

The test bench knows it flipped the gain back to nominal — that knowledge is external ground truth no measurement can match. So: feed the gain signal into the authority supervisor and add a small state machine:

1 · Arm on the edgegain returns to nominal (and diagnostic_restore_enable is on) → set restore_pending.
→
2 · Confirm 0.375 srequire 3 consecutive fast publications with no trip — a "nothing is on fire" check, not proof of health.
→
3 · Ramp 0.5 sraise a by 1.6/s (0.2→1 in four 0.125 s updates). Any trip or fresh deficit cancels within one update. Feature default-off.

Their fixed-record replay: full authority 0.81 s after the gain edge, zero false activations — versus 19.7–21.3 s for the committed blind fallback. They honestly flag that the 3-clean-fast check alone is weak (it also passes in 12 windows before the repair) — the safety rests entirely on the external edge. Status: proposal only — not implemented anywhere in the repo.

The real tension Codex's report never states. Case 4's damage lives where the command has no power: between residual-branch engagements the published margin reads healthy at cap. "Healthy readings, then quiet" after a genuine repair (this record) and "healthy readings, then quiet" with quiet-band damage still present (case 4) are observationally identical inside the law. Any rule that recovers fast on healthy-looking evidence re-probes known-bad territory in case 4 (my candidate: 51% violation); any rule cautious enough for case 4 is slow here (latch 120: stalls). That is the honest justification for using external ground truth in diagnostic tests — and simultaneously the reason it must not be a load-bearing production mechanism: real damage will not announce itself on a signal wire.

Case 8 (starts_damaged_improves) fails at HEAD in every config tried (regulation-quality criteria + recovery-time criterion trade off through the same latch knob); it passed on 07-02 and needs its own targeted look — it is a broadband case, where damage is coherently visible, so an observability-gated heal rule (§6.5) is a plausible fix for it, unlike case 4.

5 · Verdict on Codex's solution

What it gets right

· The diagnosis — both locks, exact timestamps, correct correction of its own first report (1/√2 is the metric's cap; 2.31 s measurement delay is physics).

· Layers A and B, which it (as an earlier session) had in fact already committed: the clamp guard, the feasible production config, the blind probe.

· Not resetting measurement branches; trips outrank restoration; feature-gated default-off; honest fine print about the weak fast-check and the vacuous harness.

Why I would not merge layer C

· Wrong side of the interface. It threads a test-bench signal through the Speedgoat model into a safety-critical, codegen, bitwise-equivalence-tested law — 3 new params + a state machine that is dead code in every real experiment.

· The confirmation is decorative. By its own count the 3-clean-fast condition also holds in 12 pre-repair windows; all safety hangs on one wire being correct.

· Validated with a replay that cannot fail. The no-regression evidence is an archive where the branch can't arm; meanwhile the fresh suite shows the surrounding recovery work already cost a real defense property (case 4).

· It solves a convenience problem with a permanent mechanism. 20 s of test-bench waiting does not justify production surface area.

6 · What I'd do instead

  1. Do the fast diagnostic restore in the harness, not the law. The test script is the thing that knows the gain returned — let it act on its own knowledge: after commanding gain→nominal, watch the logged trip flag for ~0.5 s, then pulse the existing enable/pause path (osi_gain_enable_initial is already an ExportedGlobal) or re-initialize the OSI state so a restarts at a_init. Same ~1 s result as layer C, zero production code touched. This is Codex's idea, relocated to where the ground truth lives. (If a harness-side reset is truly impossible on the rig, layer C gated off-by-default is an acceptable second choice — its internal design is fine.)
  2. Revert latch_decay_tau_s to ~120 s (defaults and main_ColumnSetup). The 200→1 change (smuggled into the 07-03 phase-lead commit) is what broke case 4: post-residual-shed caution is the only thing standing between the law and re-probing a hazard band it cannot see. Verified: stock law + τ=120 passes case 4 with 0% violation (9/10).
  3. Keep the committed clamp + blind probe — they are the correct general fix for both locks. Verified here: production config recovers this record autonomously. Consider having prepare_fable_runtime error instead of warn on an unreachable target for physical runs — the warning scrolled by unnoticed on 07-06.
  4. Fix the 0.98-stall wart. Snap the latch to a_max once it is within min_step/2 (or let the commit gate accept a_max − a_cont < min_step). Two lines; removes ~10 s of pointless creep at the top of every blind recovery.
  5. Law-level fast recovery, if wanted, must be observability-gated — and validated against new suite cases. My tested "healed-then-quiet" probe (remember the last valid margins; if ≥ threshold for 3 hops, probe after 1 s at the model-verified rate) cuts this record to +11.3 s — but re-breaks case 4, because it trusts healthy-at-cap readings that quiet-band damage also produces. The viable refinement is Codex's own "future extension" sharpened: release caution fast only when the band that produced the shed is coherently observable now (st-binding provenance / a symmetric heal_reason from the fast FRF statistic), and add the missing suite cases first — a residual-only damage case that truly forces res_on, and a healed-then-quiet case shaped like this record. Until that exists, blind-probe patience is the price of quiet-band safety.
