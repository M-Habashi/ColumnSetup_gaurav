Damage-Robust Phase Lead — Guide & Comparison

Why the first-order phase lead loses (and can lose stability) when the specimen degrades during the RTHS test, and how the new optimize_vHS_phase_lead_robust designs prefilters that hold their NCF margin along the whole measured damage path: a lead + lag variant (pl_7000_robust) and a lead + notch variant (pl_7000_robust_notch, the current recommendation). The engine takes an ordered list of identified damage states, so future 3k/1k identifications drop straight in. Data: sp1 healthy state (sp1_7000 + tf_sp1_7000_20260627_210411) and damaged state (sp1_5000 + tf_sp1_5000_20260627_233333), both with fem_sp1_7000, PID and phase lead fixed during the test.

1. The problem: a good initial NCF does not survive damage

The current optimizers pick the 1-pole/1-zero lead that minimizes J4 subject to the NCF margin of the healthy loop num·G_T·PID·H_dc_dm·k. That constraint only sees the initial plant. When the specimen degrades (sp1: k drops from 7201 N/mm to 5381 N/mm, 74.7 %), two things change at once:

  1. the force-feedback gain fm = k·dm scales down with k — the small, benign part; and
  2. the identified actuator plant H_dc_dm (servo + specimen) changes shape: with the softer specimen its gain rises everywhere, up to 3.9× at 40 Hz (the oil-column/specimen resonance drops in frequency and grows in peak). This is the dominant effect and it is not captured by scaling k.
Measured on the sp1 pair (closed-loop simulation with the identified damaged model): both shipped first-order leads make the damaged hybrid loop unstable — pl_7000_ncf40_v2 (healthy NCF 0.400) reaches max‑|pole| = 1.0051 and pl_7000_ncf3 (healthy NCF 0.300, J4 15.0 %) reaches 1.0114. The baseline with no lead stays stable and its NCF actually improves from 0.256 to 0.375. The aggressive lead is what damage exploits.

Why exactly the 1p1z lead fails

A first-order lead with section ratio r = p/z buys asin((r−1)/(r+1)) degrees of phase at its center — but it also multiplies the loop by r at high frequency. pl_7000_ncf40_v2 (z 2.02 Hz, p 11.5 Hz, r ≈ 5.7) needs that ratio to lift the margin at the 2.4 Hz structural critical point, and as a side effect its healthy NCF minimum moves to 37.6 Hz — a point that only stays safe because the healthy actuator gain is small there. Damage raises exactly that gain by ~4×, and the 38–45 Hz region walks into the −1 point. In a 1p1z lead the low-frequency phase and the high-frequency gain are the same knob; you cannot have the phase without arming the high-frequency trap.

Note also that a simple "scale k" robustness check is misleading here: it predicts NCF ≈ 0.45 for the old lead at the damaged state while the identified damaged model gives 0.125 — and the true loop is unstable despite the positive distance to −1 (the Nyquist winding number changes; the NCF margin alone is not a stability certificate).

2. The robust design

2.1 The damage path, not just the endpoint

Both states of sp1 are identified, so the plant change is measured: Δ(ω) = Ldamaged(ω) / Lhealthy(ω). The specimen does not jump between the two states — it degrades progressively — so the optimizer constrains the whole path

L(λ, ω) = L_healthy(ω) · Δ(ω)^λ ,   λ ∈ {0.35, 0.7, 1.0, 1.15}

(complex power via log-magnitude and unwrapped phase; λ = 1 is exactly the identified damaged loop, λ = 1.15 adds headroom beyond it). With several identified states the path becomes piecewise — healthy → 5k → 3k → 1k — anchored exactly at every identified loop (see §5). Because a real identification also has placement error, template_warp_scenarios = [0.9 1.1] re-checks the whole path with every template feature (in particular the damaged actuator resonance) moved ±10 % in frequency. Two hard constraints replace the single one:

NCF( L_healthy · G_T )      ≥ target_ncf_margin    (nominal, default 0.40)
min over λ NCF( L(λ) · G_T ) ≥ robust_ncf_target    (damage path, default 0.25)
Stability by continuity: requiring the loop to keep a finite distance from −1 at every λ on the path means the Nyquist winding number cannot change between healthy and damaged — so the path constraint is also a stability certificate for every intermediate damage state, which the single-point NCF of the old optimizer is not. The λ-grid plus the final closed-loop simulation of the identified damaged model (reported as Stable_damaged/J4_damaged) close that gap.

2.2 The filter: leads, lags, and complex-zero notches

The prefilter is G_T(s) = k_T·∏(s+z_i)/∏(s+p_i) times optional second-order sections, in the legacy format (vHS.m consumes the saved phase_lead_tf unchanged). Each first-order section may be a lead (p > z) or a lag (p < z); each optional second-order section is a notch

(s² + 2·ζ_z·ω_n·s + ω_n²) / (s² + 2·ζ_p·ω_n·s + ω_n²),   ζ_z < ζ_p

with unity DC and high-frequency gain. k_T keeps the total DC gain at 1. The idea: a lead supplies phase at the structural band; against the damaged actuator resonance you then have two tools — a lag pulls the gain down across a whole decade (blunt, forgiving, costs J4 broadband), a notch cuts only the resonance band (surgical, nearly free in J4, but must be made wide enough to survive template placement error — that is exactly what the warp scenarios enforce). normalizePhaseLeadConfig and buildPhaseLeadFilter were extended for lag and notch_sections; legacy files are unaffected.

3.1 Why the notch wins — and how it was made safe

The lead+lag pays J4 broadband: its low-frequency lag distorts the command exactly where the earthquake has energy. The notch instead cuts only the 38–45 Hz band where the damaged actuator resonance lives — the filter plot below shows the lead+notch (copper) tracking the old 1p1z's phase almost exactly through the structural band, then carving a −12 dB slot at 40 Hz. The record has almost no command energy there, so the healthy J4 barely notices (peak command amplification only 1.30×).

The catch, found by a fragility study: an unconstrained notch design tuned to the raw template collapsed from NCF 0.385 to 0.235 when the template's resonance was shifted −15 % in frequency, while the lead+lag barely moved (0.317–0.382 over ±15 %). A narrow notch is a precision instrument; it must not be trusted at its exact measured frequency. Hence template_warp_scenarios: the constraint set is replicated with the template frequency-warped (default recommendation ±10 %), which forces the optimizer to widen the notch (ζ_p 0.50 → 0.56 with a deeper cut) until the design holds everywhere. The shipped pl_7000_robust_notch satisfies all 12 warped-path scenarios.

Remaining caveat: the lead+notch keeps the lead's high-frequency gain (8.8× above ~60 Hz, like the old 1p1z's 5.7×). The NCF grid checks to 80 Hz and the closed-loop simulations are stable, but unmodeled dynamics above the identification band would see that gain. If that worries you for a given rig, add hf_gain_scenarios rows (e.g. [2 60]) or lower center_frequency_bounds_hz(2); the blunt lead+lag (HF gain 1.86) remains the conservative fallback.

4. How to run it

Quick start (sp1 defaults)

>> optimize_vHS_phase_lead_robust_ncf   % in control_and_HS/d_HS

Runs healthy = sp1_7000 with the damage-state list {5k}, the recommended structure (1 lead + 1 notch, template warps ±10 %), targets 0.40/0.25, warm-started from the previous artifact, compares against pl_7000_robust and pl_7000_ncf40_v2, and saves pl_7000_robust_notch.mat (legacy format — select it in vHS.m as usual; the saved phase_lead_tf already contains the notch). Runtime ≈ 1–2 minutes. Workspace overrides, same pattern as the other wrappers:

robust_ncf_target = 0.30;                 % ask for more damaged margin
section_count_override = 2;               % lead+lag instead of lead+notch:
notch_count_override = 0;                 %   2 first-order sections, 0 notches
phase_lead_file_name_override = 'pl_7000_robust_r30';
optimize_vHS_phase_lead_robust_ncf

Engine call and key options

config = struct( ...
    'specimen_file', '...sp1_7000.mat', ...          % healthy state
    'h_dc_dm_model_file', '...tf_sp1_7000_...mat', ...
    'fem_file', '...fem_sp1_7000.mat', ...
    'damage_states', struct( ...                     % ordered, least -> most damaged
        'label', {'5k'}, ...
        'specimen_file', {'...sp1_5000.mat'}, ...
        'h_dc_dm_model_file', {'...tf_sp1_5000_...mat'}), ...
    'target_ncf_margin', 0.40, ...     % healthy NCF floor
    'robust_ncf_target', 0.25, ...     % floor along the (warped) damage path
    'template_warp_scenarios', [0.9 1.1], ...
    'phase_lead_section_count', 1, ...
    'notch_section_count', 1);
result = optimize_vHS_phase_lead_robust(config);

Adding the future damage states (3k, 1k) when they are identified

The damage model is an ordered list, not a single pair. When a new state has been identified (specimen k,m,c from a_specimen_id, actuator model from sys_id.m), append one entry — nothing else changes:

'damage_states', struct( ...
    'label', {'5k', '3k', '1k'}, ...
    'specimen_file', {'...sp1_5000.mat', '...sp1_3000.mat', '...sp1_1000.mat'}, ...
    'h_dc_dm_model_file', {'...tf_sp1_5000_...mat', ...
                           '...tf_sp1_3000_...mat', ...
                           '...tf_sp1_1000_...mat'}), ...
'seed_phase_leads', {{'pl_7000_robust_notch.mat'}}, ...  % warm start from
                                                         % the current design

What happens internally: the damage path becomes piecewise — healthy → 5k → 3k → 1k — anchored exactly at every identified loop (path coordinate λ = 1, 2, 3 …), with template_lambda_grid fractions checked inside every segment, the >1 entries extrapolating beyond the last state, and the warp factors applied to the whole path. The report table gains NCF_3k / J4_3k / Stable_3k (and 1k) columns, and the degradation plot gains an integer marker per state. Because the previous design is passed in seed_phase_leads, the optimizer first re-checks it against the extended path (the polish restores feasibility from a near-miss before minimizing J4), so successive re-runs refine rather than restart. The same list mechanism accepts damage states from other specimens too — the constraint then reads "survive every damage trajectory measured on this rig".

Designing for a NEW specimen (only the healthy state identified)

The damage template is the transferable knowledge from sp1: "this is how this class of specimen + actuator changes when it degrades". For a new specimen sp2 where only the healthy identification exists:

config.specimen_file        = '...sp2_healthy.mat';      % new nominal
config.h_dc_dm_model_file   = '...tf_sp2_healthy...mat';
% reference damage physics measured on sp1 (all its identified states):
config.damage_states = struct( ...
    'label', {'5k'}, ...                      % + 3k, 1k when identified
    'specimen_file', {'...sp1_5000.mat'}, ...
    'h_dc_dm_model_file', {'...tf_sp1_5000...mat'});
config.template_healthy_specimen_file      = '...sp1_7000.mat';
config.template_healthy_h_dc_dm_model_file = '...tf_sp1_7000...mat';

With template_healthy_* set, the template becomes Δ = Lsp1,damaged/Lsp1,healthy (pure damage physics) applied to sp2's own healthy loop — the nominal state is sp2, the damage direction is sp1's. Since the damage amount and the shifted instability frequency of sp2 are unknown, keep λ up to 1.15 (or higher) and consider adding the generic scenarios on top (k_scale_scenarios/extra_delay_scenarios_s) for extra insurance. As more specimen pairs get tested, their templates can be added as additional scenarios — the constraint is "survive every template we have ever measured".

Honest limitation: with one measured pair, the template is one sample of "damage physics". The λ-sweep and the 1.15 headroom hedge the amount of damage, not a qualitatively different shape of change. Re-run the comparison whenever a new damaged identification exists, and treat Stable_damaged = false for any candidate lead as a hard veto.

6. Beyond the phase lead — other levers for the same three goals

  1. Robust PID co-design. The PID ([0.15 20 0.0015 512]) is chosen before the test and inherited by every design. The exact same scenario machinery can wrap a (P, D) grid or a joint PID+lead fmincon — the inner loop shapes H_dc_dm itself, so a small PID retune may buy damaged margin far more cheaply in J4 than the prefilter can.
  2. Second-order (complex) sections — DONE. Implemented as notch_section_count and it delivered exactly as hoped: J4 44.9 → 36.3 % at identical robustness targets (pl_7000_robust_notch, §3.1). The warp scenarios were the missing safety ingredient discovered on the way.
  3. Design at a detuned nominal. A one-line hedge with the existing v2 optimizer: set k_exp_r < 1 (e.g. 0.85) so the "nominal" plant sits mid-way along the expected damage path. Much weaker than the path constraint, but free.
  4. Multi-record J4. J4 is currently El Centro-specific; making the objective the worst J4 over 2–3 records (config hook earthquake_type) avoids overfitting the lag placement to one spectrum.
  5. Online λ estimate → scheduled lead (future, currently out of scope). The spectral stiffness estimator already tracks k during the test; mapping k̂ to λ and morphing between two saved robust leads (or scheduling the lag corner) would recover most of the J4 price while keeping the margin — this is the natural meeting point with the OSI/authority work once it is back in scope.
  6. Grow the template library. Each tested specimen adds one measured Δ(ω); constraining against all of them turns "robust to sp1's damage" into "robust to every damage mode this rig has ever produced". The config already accepts any number of scenarios.
