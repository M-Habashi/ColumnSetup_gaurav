# Engineering manuscript writing workflow

Version 16. Use Sol high for manuscript writing unless the author changes this preference. The user only needs to identify a passage and say to follow write.md. Gather the context yourself.

1. **Read and plan in context.** Read the section heading, its opening, neighboring paragraphs, and any figure or equation needed for the passage. Read scientific_expressions.md for terminology. For Paper 2, also read journal_paper.md and writing.md. Check relevant sources when a technical claim is uncertain. Identify what the section must establish, what the paragraph adds, and what the next sentence needs to make clear.
2. **State the immediate task before its background.** At a section opening, identify the quantity or operation the section must establish and its direct dependency. In a methods section, state that dependency before broader experimental motivation. Introduce the inputs over the next sentences if listing them in the opening would burden its subject or delay its verb. At later paragraph and sentence boundaries, establish a need only when context has not already done so. Keep each claim within the method or branch it describes. Avoid a roadmap or generic purpose statement, and let the section deliver what its opening prepares the reader to expect. Give only the methodological detail needed to assess or repeat the work, as recommended by the [MIT Communication Lab](https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/broad/commkit/journal-article-methods/).
3. **Develop a connected explanation.** State each point directly and connect it to what precedes. Give it the evidence or equations it needs. Let paragraph endings convey a result, interpretation, or next dependency rather than repeat the opening or introduce an unsupported claim. End a section by completing its stated task or making a useful handoff when needed. Within sentences, place familiar context before new information when that helps the reader and give the new point natural emphasis near the end. Use a connective only when its logical relation is real. Let the reasoning determine length and paragraph count. These checks follow [Gopen and Swan](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/comp/150FP/archive/george-gopen/sci.html) and the [Harvard Writing Center](https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/transitions).

Preserve supported facts, qualifications, notation, equations, and relevant attribution. Use the impersonal engineering-paper voice of Paper 1. State methodological choices declaratively, usually with natural passive voice. Avoid first-person phrasing and instructional imperatives. Keep a physical component active when it performs the action. Keep the core subject and verb close. If the subject bundles several technical labels, use an established shorter referent and give the remaining relation after the verb or in the next sentence. Remove redundant labels, but retain distinctions the science needs. Use established engineering terms; define any unfamiliar label that is necessary. Preserve a grammatical “where” continuation that defines symbols after an equation instead of changing it mechanically to “Here”. [Paper 1 paragraphs 22, 52, 122, 130, and 245](writing_trials/paper1_paragraphs.json) illustrate useful rhetorical moves, not required templates or transferable engineering claims. Keep the prose clear, with few commas, no semicolons, and no “whose”. Avoid suspended hyphens: write complete terms such as autospectra and cross spectra. Ordinary internal hyphens such as short-time remain acceptable.

For manuscript prose, aim for no more than 25 words per sentence, following [published journal-writing guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/pcd/issues/2018/18_0085.htm). Apply the [first-seven-words check](https://www.bu.edu/teaching-writing/resources/sentence-clarity-characters-and-actions/): the main subject and a meaningful verb should appear by about word seven, apart from a brief necessary introduction. If a long noun phrase delays the verb, use a shorter established subject and move the detail after the verb or into the next sentence. [Digital.gov](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language/writing/style) recommends unpacking strings of three or more nouns that hide their relationship. Count during review and revise sentences that miss these checks while preserving equations, technical terms, and necessary qualifications.

Reread the preceding context, revision, and following paragraph together. Check whether each new point arrives after its need is clear, each paragraph advances one point, and sentence endings carry the intended emphasis. Remove repeated phrases and restated relations while retaining necessary causal links. Check technical roles, symbol definitions, citations, and qualifications. Preserve approved wording, including grammatical equation continuations, when it remains clear and correct.

Return the revised passage in routine use, adding a short note only for a consequential issue. Edit the manuscript when asked to edit it. Before editing manuscript HTML, check its review-workflow flag and follow the version instructions in writing.md and REVIEW_WORKFLOW.md. If the flag is absent, follow writing.md's instruction to ask before editing. For a controlled trial, use its supplied context and output format.

When refining this guide, freeze a target passage with its heading, neighbors, equations, and technical facts. Give each fresh Sol-high writer the current guide and that same context, without earlier drafts or critiques. Compare the full candidate with the original for technical accuracy, section purpose, pre-verb load, sentence length, noun strings, repetition, and flow. Do not rely on the agent's self-review alone. Record a concrete failure, change as few guide words as possible, and repeat with a new writer. Keep trial agents read-only and apply accepted manuscript wording only through the version workflow.
