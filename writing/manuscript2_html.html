Online estimation of the NCF stability indicator

This section derives the normalized coprime factor online stability indicator (NCF OSI) for the outer controller loop. The estimator combines three branches: direct identification from displacement measurements, residual output-power inference, and a healthy-model prior. The following formulation develops these branches and combines their contributions into a single indicator for adaptive control.

1. Outer controller loop and NCF margin

Figure 1 shows the outer controller loop, where u(t) is the desired displacement obtained from the numerical substructure after phase-lead compensation. The signal v(t) is the command sent by the controller to the transfer system, and y(t) is the measured actuator displacement. The controller is denoted by C(s), where s is the Laplace variable. The plant H(s,k) represents the transfer system and physical substructure. It includes the actuator and servo-valve dynamics, together with control–structure interaction. The parameter k denotes the specimen stiffness.

The adaptive factor λ scales the controller output. The adaptive controller automatically updates λ based on the online stability indicator to maintain stable operation and tracking performance. The open-loop dynamics are given by

L(s,λ,k)=λC(s)H(s,k).
(1)

The plant relates the control command to the measured displacement as

H(s,k)=Y(s)V(s).
(2)

Here, Y(s) and V(s) are the Laplace transforms of y(t) and v(t), respectively. Hence, the closed-loop response is

T(s,λ,k)=Y(s)U(s)=L(s,λ,k)1+L(s,λ,k),
(3)

where U(s) is the Laplace transform of the desired displacement.

The NCF margin measures robustness to perturbations in a normalized coprime factor description [1]. For an internally stable interconnection of a general plant P(s) and controller K(s), the margin is defined by

b(P,K)=‖[K(s)I][I+P(s)K(s)]−1[IP(s)]‖∞−1.
(4)

Here, I is the identity matrix and ‖·‖∞ is the H∞ norm, defined as the largest singular value maximized over frequency. Following the SISO derivation presented by Gomaa et al. [2], this expression becomes

b(P,K)=infω|1+P(jω)K(jω)|(1+|P(jω)|2)(1+|K(jω)|2),
(5)

where ω is angular frequency and j is the imaginary unit. This form avoids a numerical singular-value calculation. For the present indicator, the forward path is grouped into P(s)=L(s,λ,k) with K(s)=1. The resulting margin is

bNCF(λ,k)=infωδ(L(jω,λ,k)).
(6)

At a given angular frequency, the substitution s=jω gives the complex open-loop response L(jω,λ,k). This value contains both the magnitude and phase of the loop response. The function δ is the normalized chordal distance between this response and the critical point −1:

δ(L(jω,λ,k))=|1+L(jω,λ,k)|2(1+|L(jω,λ,k)|2).
(7)

As the open-loop response approaches −1, the numerator approaches zero. Consequently, a smaller value of δ indicates a smaller stability margin. The three estimator branches apply the following common mapping to their respective loop-response estimates:

ϕ(L(jω,λ,k))=min{δ(L(jω,λ,k)),12}.
(8)

2. Short-time spectral estimation

The estimator is updated every Δt=0.125s, using the latest Tw=0.5s of displacement data. Let n index the update and r=0,…,N−1 index the samples within that window. The number of samples is N=fsTw, where fs is the sampling frequency. Denote the current segments by un[r] and yn[r]. Their windowed discrete Fourier transforms are

Un(ωm)=∑r=0N−1w[r]un[r]e−j2πmr/N.
(9)
Yn(ωm)=∑r=0N−1w[r]yn[r]e−j2πmr/N.
(10)

Here, m is the frequency-bin index and ωm=2πmfs/N. The Hann window is w[r]=12{1−cos[2πr/(N−1)]}. Windowing reduces the influence of discontinuities at the segment boundaries. Averaging windowed spectral estimates follows the modified-periodogram approach introduced by Welch [3].

At each frequency, the instantaneous input power and output power are accompanied by the cross-power term:

puu,n(ω)=|Un(ω)|2.
(11)
pyy,n(ω)=|Yn(ω)|2.
(12)
pyu,n(ω)=Yn(ω)Un*(ω).
(13)

The superscript * denotes complex conjugation. These are unnormalized spectral powers. The common normalization cancels in the ratios used below. To reduce fluctuations between successive windows, each term is updated through an exponentially weighted average:

p¯ab,n(ω)=(1−ηs)p¯ab,n−1(ω)+ηspab,n(ω),ab∈{uu,yy,yu}.
(14)

An overbar denotes the averaged estimate. The smoothing coefficient is ηs=0.39, and the first complete window initializes each estimate. Exponential averaging assigns progressively smaller weights to older observations [4]. It therefore provides a short memory while allowing the spectra to follow changes in the experiment.

For fs=2048Hz, each window contains 1024 samples and the bin spacing is 2Hz. The retained frequency set is

Ω={ωm:2≤fm≤100 Hz, fm∉[58,62] Hz}.
(15)
fm=ωm2π.
(16)

Excluding the line-noise interval leaves 47 frequency bins. In the following equations, ω denotes a frequency at which the estimator is evaluated. Spectra are written as functions of ω, while transfer-function values are written at s=jω. The subscript n identifies the current update and its operating condition (λn,kn), where kn is the current, generally unknown stiffness. Thus, T^n(jω) estimates the current response T(jω,λn,kn); no separate estimate of kn is required.

3. Confidence-weighted identification

The averaged spectra give a measured estimate of the closed-loop response and its magnitude-squared coherence:

T^n(jω)=p¯yu,n(ω)p¯uu,n(ω).
(17)
γn2(ω)=|p¯yu,n(ω)|2p¯uu,n(ω)p¯yy,n(ω).
(18)

The hat denotes an estimate. Coherence lies between zero and one and measures the consistency of the linear command–response relationship. Interpreting the spectral ratio as T(jω,λn,kn) assumes that the output disturbance is uncorrelated with the command. This assumption requires care in RTHS because the command may itself be influenced by feedback through the numerical substructure. High coherence is therefore supporting evidence, rather than proof of an unbiased estimate; feedback-related identification bias is discussed by Forssell and Ljung [5].

The confidence assigned to this estimate also depends on command activity. Define the root-mean-square command amplitude urms,n and a binary activity variable An by

urms,n=1N∑r=0N−1un[r]2.
(19)
An={1,urms,n>ηA,0,otherwise.
(20)

The activity threshold ηA has the same displacement units as u. Its selected value is 0.0575 in the recorded displacement units, approximately 4.54σu,0, where σu,0 is the command-noise standard deviation estimated from a quiet record.

Within an active window, a frequency bin may still contain little command energy. Its relative input power prel,n(ω) and input leverage ℓn(ω) are defined as

prel,n(ω)=p¯uu,n(ω)maxν∈Bp¯uu,n(ν).
(21)
ℓn(ω)=prel,n(ω)prel,n(ω)+pfloor.
(22)

Here, ℬ is the full 2–100 Hz analysis grid, ν is a frequency on that grid, and pfloor=10−3 is a dimensionless reference level. The leverage suppresses the influence of bins with weak input power. Combining these measures gives the confidence

cn(ω)=Anγn2(ω)ℓn(ω),0≤cn(ω)≤1.
(23)

For a bin with vanishing input power, the measured ratio is not used and its confidence is taken as zero. The confidence is a weighting factor, not a statistical confidence probability.

When measured information is limited, the estimate is drawn toward the healthy loop model. Let L0(jω)=C(jω)H(jω,k0) denote the healthy full-gain loop, where k0 is the initial specimen stiffness. At update n, the adaptive factor λn gives the prior responses

Lpr,n(jω)=λnL0(jω).
(24)
Tpr,n(jω)=Lpr,n(jω)1+Lpr,n(jω).
(25)

The subscript pr denotes the model prior. In particular, Tpr,n(jω)=T(jω,λn,k0) is the healthy-model response at the current adaptive factor. The usable closed-loop estimate is the confidence-weighted combination

T~n(jω)=cn(ω)T^n(jω)+[1−cn(ω)]Tpr,n(jω).
(26)
L^n(jω)=T~n(jω)1−T~n(jω).
(27)

The tilde denotes the blended response. The reconstructed open-loop estimate follows by solving Equation (3) for L(s,λ,k). Thus, a well-supported measurement governs the estimate, while a weakly supported measurement leaves it close to the prior.

The identified margin curve bid,n(ω) is then obtained from Equation (8). The corresponding prior curve is bpr,n(ω):

bid,n(ω)=med3{ϕ(L^n(jω))}.
(28)
bpr,n(ω)=ϕ(Lpr,n(jω)).
(29)

The operator med3 is a moving median over three adjacent frequency bins. It reduces isolated spectral irregularities. Both curves remain functions of frequency until the evidence is combined.

4. Residual output-power inference

Weak command excitation limits direct identification, but the measured response can retain evidence of a change in the loop. To establish a reference, the first three non-overlapping windows are assumed to represent healthy operation at full gain. Their median output power is

pyy,0(ω)=medianh=1,2,3|Yh(0)(ω)|2.
(30)
qobs,n(ω)=p¯yy,n(ω)pyy,0(ω).
(31)

The index h labels the reference windows, and Yh(0)(ω) is the windowed transform of the measured displacement in reference window h. The fixed reference power is denoted by pyy,0(ω). The observed ratio qobs,n(ω) measures subsequent changes relative to it.

When the desired displacement contributes little to the measured response, an equivalent additive output disturbance is filtered by 1/[1+L(jω,λn,kn)]. If the disturbance spectrum remains approximately unchanged from the healthy reference, a candidate loop λngL0(jω) predicts the output-power ratio

qmod,n(ω,g)=|1+L0(jω)1+λngL0(jω)|2.
(32)

The positive, dimensionless factor g represents an effective change in loop magnitude. It is not a direct estimate of specimen stiffness. Equation (32) also shows the assumption behind the inference: changes in disturbance intensity or command-driven response can produce output-power changes that are unrelated to stability.

At each retained frequency, a candidate is selected by matching the observed and modeled power ratios in logarithmic form:

gfit,n(ω)=argming∈𝒢|logqobs,n(ω)−logqmod,n(ω,g)|.
(33)

Here, 𝒢 contains 25 candidate values between 0.5 and 6, and log denotes the natural logarithm. Logarithmic comparison measures relative discrepancies between positive power ratios. The fit is performed independently at each frequency, so the resulting g curve is an inference model rather than a uniquely identified physical transfer function.

The fitted change is retained through a logarithmic memory. Denote the remembered value by g―n(ω), initialized at one, and let gtar,n(ω) be the target of the current update. The remembered value is updated as an exponentially weighted average in logarithmic space:

logg―n(ω)=(1−ηg)logg―n−1(ω)+ηgloggtar,n(ω).
(34)

During accumulation of residual evidence, the target is gtar,n(ω)=gfit,n(ω) and the update coefficient is ηg=0.12. Recovery is assessed using the band-median observed ratio

rΩ,n=medianω∈Ωqobs,n(ω).
(35)

When rΩ,n<1, the recovery target is one and ηg=0.27. The remembered change then decays toward its healthy value. In the absence of a fitting or recovery update, it is held. This memory allows previously detected changes to remain represented during periods of weak excitation.

The residual margin curve is evaluated using the same distance as the identified branch:

bres,n(ω)=med3{ϕ(λng―n(ω)L0(jω))}.
(36)

The subscript res denotes residual output-power inference. Using a common distance permits the two branches to be combined at the same frequency.

5. Evidence fusion and reported NCF OSI

The contribution of the residual branch depends on the size of the inferred change. Define its detection weight dn(ω) by

dn(ω)=clip[0,1](g¯n(ω)−1gdet−1).
(37)

The selected detection scale is gdet=1.75. The clipping operator limits its argument to the stated interval. The weight increases continuously from zero at g―n(ω)=1 to one at the selected detection scale gdet. This scale determines how strongly an inferred loop change contributes to the indicator.

The confidence cn(ω) assigns weight to direct identification. The residual branch receives part of the remaining weight, with any unsupported share assigned to the prior:

wid,n(ω)=cn(ω).
(38)
wres,n(ω)=[1−cn(ω)]dn(ω).
(39)
wpr,n(ω)=[1−cn(ω)][1−dn(ω)].
(40)

These three nonnegative weights sum to one. At each retained frequency, the combined distance estimate is therefore

βn(ω)=wid,n(ω)bid,n(ω)+wres,n(ω)bres,n(ω)+wpr,n(ω)bpr,n(ω).
(41)

All terms on the right-hand side are evaluated at the same frequency. The symbol βn(ω) distinguishes this combined curve from the final scalar indicator. When command activity vanishes, cn(ω)=0, but remembered residual evidence may still contribute. If neither branch supplies evidence of a change, the estimate returns to the current gain-scaled prior. Such a value remains model-supported rather than newly measured.

Frequency reduction is performed after fusion. Sort the 47 retained values of βn(ω) in ascending order and denote the second-smallest value by βn,(2). The instantaneous indicator is

binst,n=βn,(2)=βn(ω*,n),
(42)

where ω*,n is the selected frequency. This reduction discards one lowest value so that a single low bin does not govern the complete estimate. It is a deliberate robustness choice in the estimator, and it can also suppress a genuine narrow frequency minimum.

Finally, the reported NCF OSI is obtained by smoothing the instantaneous value in time:

bOSI,n=(1−ηb)bOSI,n−1+ηbbinst,n,0<ηb≤1.
(43)

The coefficient ηb specifies the fraction of the newest estimate admitted at each update. Temporal smoothing reduces fluctuations, but introduces a delay in following abrupt changes.

The resulting NCF OSI tracks changes in the stability margin within the monitored frequency band. Its interpretation depends on the available excitation and the healthy-model prior. Residual inference also depends on the assumed disturbance spectrum. Frequency filtering and the removal of one lowest value mean that the reported indicator is not a guaranteed lower bound on the full-frequency NCF margin. The adaptive controller uses this indicator to update λn during the experiment.
