2. Online NCF stability indicator framework for adaptive control

Section draft rewritten from manuscript2 for journal submission. Readers are assumed to be familiar with Ref. [2].

The normalized coprime factor online stability indicator (NCF OSI) introduced in the authors’ previous study [2] quantifies the robustness of the actuator control loop in hybrid simulation to degradation of the specimen. This section describes how the indicator is estimated during the test from the desired displacement calculated by the numerical substructure and the measured actuator displacement. Three sources of evidence are combined at each frequency. The first is direct identification, bid, of the closed loop from the measured signals. The second is output-power inference, bpow, which detects a change in the loop from the change in measured output power. The third is the prior identification of the initial state, b0, obtained before the test. Moreover, to keep the loop stable with an adequate margin as the specimen degrades while retaining as much tracking performance as possible, the controller output is scaled by the adaptive factor λ, which is updated during the test from the estimated indicator. The derivation of the SISO margin and its relation to specimen damage are given in [2] and are restated here only as required by the estimator.

2.1Control loop and stability margin

Figure 1 shows the hybrid simulation loop with the adaptive controller. The numerical substructure calculates the desired displacement d(t), which is then passed through the phase-lead compensator to obtain u(t). The controller C(s) forms a command that is scaled by the adaptive factor λ∈(0,1] and applied as v(t) to the plant H(s,k). The plant comprises the transfer system (servo-valve and actuator) and the specimen of stiffness k, and therefore includes control–structure interaction. The measured actuator displacement is y(t), and the measured force fm(t) is returned to the numerical substructure.

With H(s,k)=Y(s)/V(s), the loop and closed-loop transfer functions are

L(s,λ,k)=λC(s)H(s,k),
(1)
T(s,λ,k)=Y(s)U(s)=L(s,λ,k)1+L(s,λ,k),
(2)

where U(s) and Y(s) are the Laplace transforms of u(t) and y(t). As in [2], the NCF margin [1] is evaluated with the plant P=L and unit feedback K=1, for which the general definition reduces to

bNCF(λ,k)=infωδ(L(jω,λ,k)),
(3)
δ(L)=|1+L|2(1+|L|2).
(4)

The function δ is the chordal distance between L(jω) and the critical point −1. All branches of the estimator evaluate the capped distance

ϕ(L)=min{δ(L),12}.
(5)

Because |1+L|2−(1+|L|2)=2ReL, the distance exceeds δ(0)=1/2 only where ReL>0, that is, where the loop response lies far from the critical point. The cap removes credit from these frequencies and bounds the reported indicator by 1/2.

2.2Short-time spectral estimation

The estimator is updated every Δt=0.125s from the most recent Tw=0.5s of u and y, so consecutive windows overlap by 75%. At fs=2048Hz, each window holds N=fsTw=1024 samples and the bin spacing is 2 Hz. With n indexing the update and r=0,…,N−1 the samples in the window, the Hann-windowed discrete Fourier transforms are

Xn(ωm)=∑r=0N−1w[r]xn[r]e−j2πmr/N,x∈{u,y},
(6)

where w[r]=12{1−cos[2πr/(N−1)]} and ωm=2πmfs/N. The instantaneous auto- and cross-spectra

puu,n=|Un|2,pyy,n=|Yn|2,pyu,n=YnUn*
(7)

are unnormalized, because the normalization cancels in every ratio used below. The argument ωm is omitted for brevity. Following the averaging of modified periodograms [3] with exponential weighting [4], each spectrum is smoothed recursively and initialized by the first complete window:

p¯ab,n(ω)=(1−ηs)p¯ab,n−1(ω)+ηspab,n(ω),ab∈{uu,yy,yu}.
(8)

The analysis set retains the 2–100 Hz band and rejects line noise:

Ω={ωm:2≤fm≤100 Hz,fm∉[58,62] Hz},fm=ωm2π,
(9)

which leaves 47 bins. Hereafter ω∈Ω, and the subscript n denotes the operating condition (λn,kn) at update n. The current stiffness kn is unknown and is not estimated.

2.3Direct identification branch

The averaged spectra give the closed-loop estimate and the magnitude-squared coherence

T^n(jω)=p¯yu,n(ω)p¯uu,n(ω),γn2(ω)=|p¯yu,n(ω)|2p¯uu,n(ω)p¯yy,n(ω).
(10)

The ratio is an unbiased estimate of T(jω,λn,kn) only if the output disturbance is uncorrelated with u. This is not guaranteed in hybrid simulation, because u is computed from the measured force through the numerical substructure. High coherence is therefore treated as supporting evidence rather than proof of an unbiased estimate [5].

The weight given to T^n combines three factors. Command activity is detected from the window RMS urms,n=(N−1∑run[r]2)1/2:

An={1,urms,n>ηA,0,otherwise.
(11)

The threshold ηA is approximately 4.54σu,0, where σu,0 is the command-noise standard deviation measured in a quiet record. Within an active window, bins that carry little command energy are discounted through the input leverage

ℓn(ω)=prel,n(ω)prel,n(ω)+pfloor,prel,n(ω)=p¯uu,n(ω)maxν∈ℬp¯uu,n(ν),
(12)

where ℬ is the full 2–100 Hz grid. The confidence

cn(ω)=Anγn2(ω)ℓn(ω),0≤cn(ω)≤1,
(13)

is a weighting factor, not a probability. Where it is small, the measurement is replaced by the initial-state prior at the current adaptive factor. With L0(jω)=C(jω)H(jω,k0) the full-gain loop identified at the initial stiffness k0, the prior is

Lpr,n(jω)=λnL0(jω),Tpr,n(jω)=Lpr,n(jω)1+Lpr,n(jω).
(14)

Measurement and prior are blended in the closed-loop domain, where the spectral estimate is formed, and the loop is recovered by inverting (2):

T~n(jω)=cnT^n(jω)+(1−cn)Tpr,n(jω),L^n(jω)=T~n(jω)1−T~n(jω).
(15)

A well-supported measurement therefore governs L^n, while a weakly supported one leaves it close to the prior. The identified and prior margin curves are

bid,n(ω)=med3{ϕ(L^n(jω))},b0,n(ω)=ϕ(Lpr,n(jω)),
(16)

where med3 is a three-bin moving median over frequency that suppresses isolated spectral outliers.

2.4Output-power inference branch

When the command is weak, T^n is unreliable, but the measured output power still reflects the loop. The first three non-overlapping windows are assumed to represent the initial loop at full gain and define the initial reference power and the observed ratio

pyy,0(ω)=medianh=1,2,3|Yh(0)(ω)|2,qobs,n(ω)=p¯yy,n(ω)pyy,0(ω),
(17)

where Yh(0) is the transform of y in reference window h. If y is dominated by an equivalent output disturbance whose spectrum is unchanged from the reference period, the output is shaped by the sensitivity 1/(1+L), and a candidate loop λngL0 predicts the ratio

qmod,n(ω,g)=|1+L0(jω)1+λngL0(jω)|2.
(18)

The positive scalar g is an effective change in loop magnitude, not an estimate of k. It is fitted in each bin by matching the ratios on a logarithmic scale:

gfit,n(ω)=arg ming∈𝒢|logqobs,n(ω)−logqmod,n(ω,g)|,
(19)

where 𝒢 holds 25 logarithmically spaced values from 0.5 to 6. Because each bin is fitted independently, the curve g(ω) is an inference model rather than an identified transfer function. The fitted change is retained in a logarithmic memory initialized at g¯0=1:

logg¯n(ω)=(1−ηg)logg¯n−1(ω)+ηgloggtar,n(ω).
(20)

While the command is active (An=1), the target is gtar,n=gfit,n. Recovery is judged from the band-median ratio

rΩ,n=medianω∈Ωqobs,n(ω).
(21)

When rΩ,n<1, the output power has fallen below the initial reference, and the memory decays toward g¯=1 with gtar,n=1 and ηg replaced by ηr. Otherwise g¯n is held, so a detected change remains represented while the excitation is weak. The output-power margin curve uses the same capped distance as (16):

bpow,n(ω)=med3{ϕ(λng¯n(ω)L0(jω))}.
(22)

2.5Per-frequency fusion and reported indicator

The output-power branch is trusted in proportion to the size of the inferred change,

dn(ω)=clip[0,1](g¯n(ω)−1gdet−1),
(23)

which rises linearly from zero at g¯n=1 to one at g¯n=gdet. The three branches are weighted at each frequency by

wid,n=cn,wpow,n=(1−cn)dn,w0,n=(1−cn)(1−dn),
(24)

which are nonnegative and sum to one. Direct identification receives the share supported by the measurement, the output-power branch receives the remainder in proportion to its detection weight, and the prior receives what neither measurement supports (Table 2). The fused margin curve is

βn(ω)=wid,nbid,n(ω)+wpow,nbpow,n(ω)+w0,nb0,n(ω).
(25)

Fusion is performed bin by bin, before any reduction over frequency, so margins evaluated at different frequencies are never combined. The idle case needs no special treatment: when the command is inactive, cn=0 in every bin, an unchanged loop reads its gain-scaled prior, and a bin with a remembered change continues to report it.

The instantaneous indicator is the second-smallest value of βn over Ω,

binst,n=βn,(2)=βn(ω*,n),
(26)

where ω*,n is the governing frequency. Discarding the lowest bin prevents a single outlier from governing the estimate, at the cost of attenuating a genuine minimum that is only one bin wide. The reported indicator is smoothed in time,

bOSI,n=(1−ηb)bOSI,n−1+ηbbinst,n,
(27)

and is used by the adaptive law to update λn+1. Figure 2 summarizes one update, and Table 1 lists the estimator parameters.

2.6Assumptions and limitations

  1. The identification branch assumes that the output disturbance is uncorrelated with u. Feedback through the numerical substructure can violate this assumption and bias T^n even at high coherence [5].
  2. The output-power branch assumes that the disturbance spectrum is unchanged from the reference period and that the first 1.5 s represent the initial loop at full gain. A change in disturbance intensity or in the command-driven response can alter qobs without any change in stability.
  3. The factor g is real, so the output-power branch represents changes in loop magnitude but not in loop phase.
  4. Band limitation, trimming, and temporal smoothing mean that bOSI is not a guaranteed lower bound on the full-frequency margin bNCF, and it follows abrupt changes with a delay set by ηb.
