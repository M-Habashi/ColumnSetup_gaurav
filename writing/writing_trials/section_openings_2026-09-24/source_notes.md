# Section and paragraph openings: source and example note

- [MIT Communication Lab, Journal Article Methods](https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/broad/commkit/journal-article-methods/) says methods should explain the reason for a choice as well as enough operation for replication. The depth should suit specialist readers. It does not impose an opening formula.
- [Harvard Writing Center, Anatomy of a Body Paragraph](https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/anatomy-body-paragraph) supports a clear main point, development, and an ending that adds understanding. Its essay pattern is a rhetorical aid rather than a required sequence for derivations.
- [Harvard Writing Center, Transitions](https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/transitions) treats a transition as a real relation between ideas. Familiar context can lead to new information. A connective cannot supply missing logic.
- [Gopen and Swan, The Science of Scientific Writing](https://mathstat.tcnj.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/200/2011/08/The-Science-of-Scientific-Writing-.pdf) explains why familiar information at a sentence start and intended emphasis near its end can aid readers. These are reader expectations, not sentence rules.

Paper 1 examples in [paper1_paragraphs.json](../paper1_paragraphs.json): paragraph 22 introduces the system before components and scope, though its long sentence and punctuation are not style models. Paragraph 52 links the need for a short-window approximation to its operation and qualification. Paragraph 122 states the purpose of a study before its design. Paragraph 130 connects previous work to a new section task. Paragraph 245 identifies the physical model before its sources and details. Paragraph 68 combines context, purpose, and assumptions, but is longer than this guide should prescribe. These examples show different section jobs and do not license importing Paper 1 claims into Paper 2.

For the current Section 2.2, the supplied v22 passage moves from a degradation rationale directly to update intervals and Fourier transforms. Its own equations produce windowed transforms, auto- and cross-spectra, and recursively averaged spectra over selected bins. The plant-response identification begins in Section 2.3. The opening guidance therefore needs to establish what short-time spectral estimation does and why it supplies this changing system before procedural settings are given.

Two fresh trials exposed the practical gap in the first proposal. One kept a degradation-first opening and introduced the method only after that supporting cause. The other named the method but compressed its role into a broad claim about frequency-resolved evidence before moving to update timing. Neither explained the actual spectral operations before settings. Both changed a grammatical “where” continuation defining equation symbols to “Here”, and both self-reviews reported success. The revised guide therefore asks the writer to inspect the actual first sentence and paragraph before parameters or equations, name quantities and operations, and preserve sound equation continuations. It gives no specimen opening for copying.
