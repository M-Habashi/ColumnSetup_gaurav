## Mechanical constraints

All four candidates contain no semicolon and no occurrence of “whose.” No candidate sentence contains three or more prose commas. In each candidate, the sentence about interaction contains two commas around “including the controller.” No comma-density flags or exceptions are needed.

No candidate introduces a Paper 2 adaptive factor or claims that the paper experimentally validates an indicator or intervention.

## Recurring problems and assessment limits

The recurring substantive risk in the rejected candidates is replacing an explicitly limited claim with a stronger assertion: Candidate A adds a direction to the mass dependence, while Candidate D converts conditional, system-dependent observations into universal behavior. Candidate C presents a separate terminology problem by renaming an established system without explanation. These defects should not be conflated with ordinary changes in sentence order or the use of short sentences.

Candidate B is the strongest draft under the supplied criteria. Candidate C also passes the numerical acceptance rule because that rule permits a single terminology deduction. Its acceptance does not mean that its terminology is preferable.

This assessment is limited to meaning, context, terminology, readability, and mechanical constraints in the supplied packet. It does not independently validate the proposed laboratory mechanism or infer a mass-response relationship from external evidence. All four candidates were assessed, and none was rewritten.
