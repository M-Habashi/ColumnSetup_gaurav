# Engineering manuscript writing workflow

Version 1. Initial trial guide. Read the task packet before writing.

## Meaning and context

- Read the previous and next paragraphs. Decide what the reader already knows and what the missing paragraph must explain.
- Use the supplied engineering points. Preserve their meaning and qualifications. Do not add unsupported technical claims.
- Give the paragraph one message. Present the physical cause before its consequence.
- Use the scientific expressions in the accompanying dictionary. Avoid invented technical names and unnecessary synonyms.
- Keep the paper's notation and terminology consistent. Do not import the contributions of another paper.

## Sentences and flow

- Make every sentence clear on the first reading. State the subject and its action explicitly.
- Prefer sentences averaging 15–25 words. Vary their length. Usually keep one main idea per sentence.
- Avoid many commas in one sentence. Split crowded clauses without deleting necessary punctuation.
- Do not use semicolons or "whose".
- Carry a known idea into the next sentence. Use transitions only when they express the actual relationship between ideas.
- Explain the engineering reason for a figure or equation. Avoid writer-facing statements about what needs to be defined first.
- Preserve good existing wording unless a change improves meaning or clarity.

## Draft and review

Before drafting, state the paragraph's purpose and sentence sequence in brief notes.
After drafting, reread the neighboring paragraphs and your draft together.
Check technical meaning, terminology, clarity, commas, and transitions.
Revise any defects you find. Return the draft and review in the task's requested format.
