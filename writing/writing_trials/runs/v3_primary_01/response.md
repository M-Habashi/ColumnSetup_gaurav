## First draft

Laboratory observations suggest a physical mechanism for the gain amplification associated with stiffness degradation. The controller is initially tuned to overcome the relatively high specimen stiffness and achieve the required tracking performance. As the specimen loses stiffness, it becomes easier to move, and the same corrective control action is likely to produce greater overshoot and a larger displacement response than under the initial stiffness condition. Specimen mass also influences the extent of this response amplification. The interaction between the degraded specimen and the transfer system, including the controller, can eventually drive the system toward instability. This behavior is not universal, because the dominant instability mechanism and the performance of stability indicators can vary substantially with both transfer-system and specimen dynamics.
