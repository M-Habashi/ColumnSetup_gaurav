# Purpose

Explain how stiffness degradation may amplify the displacement response and threaten stability while clarifying that the mechanism and indicator performance depend on the system dynamics.

# Sequence

- Connect the preceding gain-amplification interpretation to laboratory observations.
- Establish the controller tuning under the initial stiffness condition.
- Explain how stiffness loss changes the response to the same corrective control action.
- Include specimen mass and the interaction that can lead toward instability.
- Qualify the mechanism and stability-indicator performance before the study aims.

# First draft

Laboratory observations suggest that the gain amplification described above can arise from the interaction between the specimen and the transfer system. Initially, the controller is tuned to overcome the relatively high specimen stiffness and achieve the required tracking performance. Stiffness loss makes the specimen easier to move. The same corrective control action is then likely to produce more overshoot and a larger displacement response than under the initial stiffness condition. Specimen mass also influences the extent of this response amplification. As degradation progresses, the interaction between the specimen and the transfer system, including the controller, can eventually drive the system toward instability. However, this behavior is not universal. The dominant instability mechanism and the performance of stability indicators can vary substantially with both transfer-system and specimen dynamics.
