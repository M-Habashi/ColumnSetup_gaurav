## First draft

Laboratory observations suggest that this gain amplification can arise when specimen stiffness degrades after the controller has been tuned. The controller is initially tuned to overcome the relatively high specimen stiffness and achieve the required tracking performance. As the specimen loses stiffness, it becomes easier to move, and the same corrective control action is likely to produce more overshoot and a larger displacement response than under the initial stiffness condition. The specimen mass also influences the extent of this response amplification. Interaction between the degraded specimen and the transfer system, including the controller, can eventually drive the system toward instability. However, this behavior is not universal. The dominant instability mechanism and the performance of stability indicators can vary substantially with both transfer-system and specimen dynamics.
