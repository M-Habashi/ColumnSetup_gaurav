# Purpose

Explain the degradation mechanism suggested by laboratory observations and connect its dependence on system dynamics to the need to consider different instability mechanisms.

- Connect stiffness loss to the likely increase in overshoot and displacement response, then identify specimen mass as an influence.
- Explain how interaction with the transfer system and controller can eventually lead toward instability.
- Prepare for the next paragraph's broader indicator objective by preserving the dependence of mechanisms and indicator performance on system dynamics.

# First draft

Laboratory observations suggest a physical mechanism for the gain amplification associated with specimen degradation. The controller is initially tuned to overcome the relatively high specimen stiffness and meet the required tracking performance. As the specimen loses stiffness, it becomes easier to move under the same corrective control action. This action is likely to produce more overshoot and a larger displacement response than under the initial stiffness condition. The extent of this response amplification also depends on specimen mass. The interaction between the degraded specimen and the transfer system can eventually drive the system toward instability. However, this behavior is not universal. Both the dominant instability mechanism and the performance of stability indicators can vary substantially with transfer-system and specimen dynamics.

# Final paragraph

Laboratory observations suggest a physical mechanism for the gain amplification associated with specimen degradation. The controller is initially tuned to overcome the relatively high specimen stiffness and meet the required tracking performance. Stiffness loss makes the specimen easier to move. Maintaining the same corrective control action is therefore likely to produce more overshoot and a larger displacement response than under the initial stiffness condition. The extent of this response amplification also depends on specimen mass. The interaction between the degraded specimen and the transfer system, including the controller, can eventually drive the system toward instability. This behavior is not universal. Both the dominant instability mechanism and the performance of stability indicators can vary substantially with transfer-system and specimen dynamics.
