## Purpose

Explain the mechanism suggested by laboratory observations for degradation-related response amplification while preserving its dependence on transfer-system and specimen dynamics.

## Sequence

- The preceding paragraph establishes that the indicator trends suggest gain amplification in the outer controller loop rather than a dominant delay mechanism.
- This paragraph must connect initial controller tuning to the specimen's response after stiffness loss and explain why that interaction can approach instability.
- The next paragraph needs the reader to understand that instability mechanisms and indicator performance depend on the system dynamics.
- Present the laboratory interpretation, initial tuning, stiffness loss and amplified response, specimen mass, interaction with the transfer system, and limits on generality.

## First draft

Laboratory observations suggest a physical explanation for this gain amplification. The controller is initially tuned to overcome the relatively high specimen stiffness and achieve the required tracking performance. As the specimen loses stiffness, it becomes easier to move under the same corrective control action. This action is therefore likely to produce greater overshoot and a larger displacement response than under the initial stiffness condition. Specimen mass also influences the extent of this response amplification. Interaction between the degraded specimen and the transfer system, including its controller, can eventually drive the system toward instability. This behavior is not universal because both the dominant instability mechanism and stability-indicator performance can vary substantially with transfer-system and specimen dynamics.
