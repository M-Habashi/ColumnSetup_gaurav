## First draft
Laboratory observations suggest a physical mechanism that is consistent with this gain amplification. The controller is initially tuned to overcome the relatively high specimen stiffness and meet the required tracking performance. As the specimen loses stiffness, it becomes easier to move. With the same corrective control action, the degraded specimen is likely to exhibit more overshoot and a larger displacement response than under its initial stiffness condition. Specimen mass also influences how much the response is amplified. The interaction between the degraded specimen and the transfer system including the controller can eventually drive the system toward instability. This behavior is not universal, because the dominant instability mechanism and the performance of stability indicators can vary substantially with transfer-system and specimen dynamics.
