# Purpose

Explain how stiffness degradation can amplify the displacement response and promote instability while preserving the dependence of this mechanism on transfer-system and specimen dynamics.

- Sentence order: laboratory observations and initial tuning, stiffness loss and amplified response, mass dependence, interaction leading toward instability, and limits to generality.

# First draft

Laboratory observations suggest a physical mechanism consistent with this gain amplification. The controller is initially tuned to overcome the relatively high specimen stiffness and achieve the required tracking performance. When the specimen loses stiffness, it becomes easier to move, so the same corrective control action is likely to produce greater overshoot and a larger displacement response than under the initial stiffness condition. The extent of this response amplification also depends on specimen mass. The resulting interaction between the degraded specimen and the transfer system, including the controller, can eventually drive the system toward instability. However, this behavior is not universal. The dominant instability mechanism and the performance of stability indicators can vary substantially with both transfer-system and specimen dynamics.
