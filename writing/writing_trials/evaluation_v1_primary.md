# Independent paragraph evaluation

All three candidates were assessed against the actual Paper 1 paragraph, its supplied neighbors, the six required ideas, and the fixed scoring criteria. No other files, trial history, or external sources were consulted. The actual paragraph was treated as the authority for meaning rather than as a sentence or style template.

## Candidate A

| Dimension | Score | Basis |
|---|---:|---|
| Completeness | 2/2 | Retains all six required ideas, including mass dependence without a specified direction and the full final caveat. |
| Fidelity | 2/2 | Preserves the causal chain and its qualifications without adding a validation claim or another mechanism. |
| Context and sequence | 2/2 | Connects the preceding gain-amplification discussion to the physical explanation, then closes with a caveat that supports the following general motivation. |
| Terminology | 2/2 | Uses clear physical and control terms and explicitly includes the controller within the transfer system. |
| Readability | 2/2 | The sentences are clear and connected. The one sentence with three commas remains easy to parse for the specific reasons below. |

**Total: 10/10. Accepted.** Completeness and fidelity both meet the required score of 2. No deductions.

Concrete comparisons with the actual paragraph:

- “Laboratory observations suggest a physical mechanism for this gain amplification” appropriately frames the explanation as suggested by observation. “This gain amplification” has a clear antecedent in the preceding paragraph. It does not claim experimental validation of an indicator or intervention.
- “Overcome the relatively high specimen stiffness and achieve the required tracking performance” preserves both initial tuning requirements.
- The paired statements “Stiffness loss makes the specimen easier to move” and “likely to produce more overshoot and a larger displacement response than under the initial stiffness condition” retain the explanation, the probability qualification, and the baseline comparison.
- “Specimen mass also influences the extent of this response amplification” preserves the source's nondirectional mass dependence. The following interaction sentence retains the degraded-specimen/transfer-system pathway to possible instability.
- “This behavior is not universal” is followed by an explicit statement that both the dominant mechanism and indicator performance can vary substantially with both systems' dynamics. The caveat therefore retains its full scope.

**Comma flag, resolved:** Sentence 6 contains three prose commas: “As degradation progresses, the interaction between the specimen and the transfer system, including the controller, can eventually drive the system toward instability.” The first comma closes a short introductory clause. The remaining pair encloses the brief clarification “including the controller.” There is one main proposition, and the inclusion is clear. This is a specific readability justification for retaining 2/2 rather than an unexamined exception.

No semicolons or instances of “whose” occur. An editor could optionally combine two of the shorter sentences, but their current length does not create a substantive readability defect.

**Total: 10/10. Accepted.** Completeness and fidelity both meet the required score of 2. No deductions.

Concrete comparisons with the actual paragraph:

- “Laboratory observations suggest how specimen stiffness degradation can amplify the displacement response and increase the risk of instability” preserves the observational basis and qualified mechanism. It makes no claim about experimentally validating the study's proposed indicator or intervention.
- “Initially tuned to overcome the relatively high specimen stiffness while maintaining the required tracking performance” retains both tuning objectives. “Maintaining” is ordinary wording for satisfying the tracking requirement in this context and does not materially change the source's meaning.

- The final two sentences preserve nonuniversality and substantial variation in both mechanism and indicator performance with the dynamics of both the transfer system and specimen.

No sentence has three or more prose commas. No semicolons or instances of “whose” occur. The opening previews points subsequently explained, but that normal explanatory structure does not warrant a repetition penalty.

## Recurring problems and assessment limits

No recurring substantive problems were found. All candidates preserve the required content, causal relationships, uncertainty, and caveat scope. None introduces a Paper 2 adaptive factor or claims experimental validation of an indicator or intervention. Candidate A's single three-comma sentence was explicitly flagged and resolved through a specific readability justification.

All candidates were assessed. The equal scores reflect the fixed rubric, not a requirement that the drafts be identical in editorial preference. This review evaluates the supplied paragraph and immediate context only. It does not independently verify the laboratory observations or the underlying physical mechanism, and it does not assess consistency with manuscript material absent from the packet.
