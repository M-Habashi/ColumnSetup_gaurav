# Engineering manuscript writing workflow

Version 6 candidate. This file is the entry point for improving a manuscript paragraph. It adds a section-level contribution check and a separate deletion pass to version 4. Trial record: writing_trials/redundancy_2026-09-24/report.md.

## Everyday use

The user only needs to identify or paste a paragraph and say to follow this file. Do the preparation yourself. Do not require the user to supply a task packet, engineering bullets, a dictionary, or a description of the workflow.

1. Locate the paragraph in the identified manuscript. Read from the relevant section opening through the target and the next paragraph. Look farther back when a definition or explanation has already been introduced. Inspect the relevant figure or equation when the explanation depends on it.
2. Read [scientific_expressions.md](scientific_expressions.md). For Paper 2, also read [journal_paper.md](journal_paper.md) and [writing.md](writing.md). These paths are relative to this file. Follow this file for drafting and review, and use the other files for paper-specific context and terminology.
3. Recover the intended message and required engineering facts from the paragraph and its context. Prepare your own brief content notes. Improve what the reader learns and the order of explanation, not just the wording. Preserve supported facts and qualifications. Do not invent a new claim to give the paragraph a stronger purpose.
4. Apply the drafting and review steps below. Resolve questions from the available manuscript and sources first. Ask one focused question only if missing information or conflicting engineering claims prevent a faithful revision.
5. By default, present only the improved paragraph. Add a short note only when a meaningful change or unresolved issue needs the user's attention. Keep planning notes, first drafts, review details, and comma counts out of the default response. Show them when requested or when a trial packet requires them.

Follow the user's requested delivery: edit the manuscript when asked to edit it, or return a proposed revision when asked for wording. Routine use does not require another tuning experiment or model comparison. Use the requested model when one is specified. Otherwise use the current agent.

For a controlled trial with a supplied task packet, use that packet's context and output format instead of gathering additional context. Keep trials isolated as instructed.

## Meaning and context

- Read the previous and next paragraphs. Decide what the reader already knows and what the missing paragraph must explain.
- Use the supplied engineering points. Preserve their meaning and qualifications across the passage and its context. An editing task does not require repeating every fact from the original paragraph when the surrounding text already supplies it. Do not add unsupported technical claims.
- Treat bullet points as content notes rather than finished sentences. Their grammar and phrasing may be poor. Recover the engineering meaning before choosing the wording.
- Give the paragraph one message. Present the physical cause before its consequence.
- Use the scientific expressions in the accompanying dictionary. Avoid invented technical names and unnecessary synonyms.
- Keep the paper's notation and terminology consistent. Do not import the contributions of another paper.
- Treat every qualification as part of the technical meaning. Keep distinctions such as "can", "is likely to", and "is typically accurate". Preserve what each qualification applies to.
- Use the dictionary to name the actual object. Do not turn a physical interaction into a hardware component or replace a named controller with a vague label.

## Sentences and flow

- Make every sentence clear on the first reading. State the subject and its action explicitly.
- Check that the subject actually performs the stated action. Use the passive voice when the component receives that action. Prefer standard scientific verb phrases from the dictionary to forced active constructions.
- Prefer sentences averaging 15–25 words. Vary their length. Usually keep one main idea per sentence.
- Avoid many commas in one sentence. Split crowded clauses without deleting necessary punctuation.
- In the final paragraph, count the prose commas in each sentence. Rewrite sentences with three or more. Remove unnecessary introductory clauses or move parenthetical information into a complete sentence. Keep the relationship between the ideas explicit.
- Do not respond to this preference by chaining a long list with "and" or writing disconnected fragments. A necessary component inventory can span connected sentences about the components' roles.
- Do not use semicolons or "whose".
- Carry a known idea into the next sentence. Use transitions only when they express the actual relationship between ideas.
- Use a pronoun when its referent is unambiguous. Repeating a full technical name in adjacent sentences is not automatically clearer.
- Explain the engineering reason for a figure or equation when the reader does not already have it. A new equation does not require another statement of the section motivation. Interpret the equation when that adds physical meaning or a useful consequence, rather than automatically translating it into words.
- Preserve good existing wording unless a change improves meaning or clarity.

## Contribution and repetition

Before drafting, make brief private notes in two groups: already established (with the earlier location) and new here (definitions, distinctions, assumptions, qualifications, or consequences still needed). Identify any short connection needed between them.

Build the draft from the "new here" notes and those necessary connections. Do not rewrite the original sentence by sentence: that can preserve its redundant structure even when the wording improves. A statement in the original is evidence to assess, not an obligation to repeat it. Preserve explicitly required content.

A general purpose already given for a method or quantity does not become new information merely because a later sentence names its symbol or formula. Keep a genuinely new technical definition or scope qualification, but introduce it directly. For example, "the filter suppresses noise" adds nothing after "the filter reduces measurement noise" unless the later sentence explains a new condition, mechanism, or limitation.

- Judge repetition by meaning, not matching words. Rephrasing an earlier explanation still repeats it. Reusing a technical term is often necessary and is not itself redundancy.
- Make each sentence add information or provide a necessary connection. If deleting a sentence leaves the reasoning and references clear, and its meaning is already available, delete it or retain only the needed connecting phrase.
- Give each explanation one place in the revised span. When prose surrounds an equation, state its purpose and interpretation where they help, without explaining the same reason on both sides.
- Preserve genuinely new distinctions, assumptions, definitions, and qualifications even when they mention a familiar concept. Do not shorten by deleting engineering meaning.
- Read the revised text as part of the section, not as a standalone explanation. Do not add a recap or concluding restatement simply to make the edited paragraph self-contained.
- No word-count reduction is a target. Fewer words count as an improvement only when the same necessary meaning remains clear.

## Draft and review

Before drafting, answer three questions in brief editorial notes: What does the preceding paragraph establish? What engineering point must this paragraph add? What does the next paragraph need the reader to understand?
State the resulting purpose and sentence sequence in the requested format.

After drafting, reread the neighboring paragraphs and your draft together. Check the actual sentences rather than reporting general compliance.

1. Map every required new idea to a sentence. For an idea removed as redundant, identify where the unchanged context already supplies it. Check that no new fact or qualification was lost.
2. Check each causal statement against the supplied facts. Delete unsupported additions. Naming a term in the dictionary does not supply evidence for a claim about it.
3. Check every sentence against the section context and the rest of the revised span. Identify the new information or necessary connection it supplies. Remove repeated explanations, including the same claim expressed in different words. Check the first and last sentences again in context.
4. Inspect technical names, verbs, and pronouns. Keep the referent clear and reuse established names when a synonym adds no distinction. Check who performs each action and whether any awkward wording was copied from the input notes.
5. Count prose commas in every final sentence. Record the counts in the review notes. Resolve any sentence with three or more before returning it.
6. Perform a separate deletion pass after the meaning check. For each final sentence, state in the private review notes the specific information that would disappear if it were deleted. Check both earlier context and the rest of the revised span. If only an already-established purpose or explanation disappears, delete the sentence or reduce it to the necessary connection. "Provides context", "improves flow", and "reinforces the point" are not sufficient reasons to keep a recap. For a necessary connection, identify the referent or reasoning step that would otherwise become unclear.
7. Reread the result and recheck the protected new definitions and qualifications. Ensure that deleting or splitting a sentence has not detached a condition, left an equation symbol undefined, or broken the connection to the next paragraph. Keep equations unchanged when the task requires it.

Record specific edits or unresolved defects. Do not claim that a check passed when it was not performed. For trials or an explicitly requested detailed review, return the first draft and final paragraph so the effect of review can be inspected. Otherwise follow the concise everyday-use output above.

## Acceptance

Correctness and terminology are requirements rather than points that can be offset by fluent prose. Revise any unsupported claim, lost qualification, misnamed component, or unnecessary invented technical name before accepting the paragraph.
Check awkward verb phrases even when their intended meaning is recoverable. An agent's positive review is evidence to inspect, not a substitute for these checks.
A fluent paragraph still needs revision if it repeats an established explanation without a necessary connection. Record a specific repeated claim and its earlier location rather than a general claim that the prose is concise.
Ordinary variation in connective wording is allowed. Distinguish a concrete defect from an optional stylistic preference.
